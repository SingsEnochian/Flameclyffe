# Arcsweep Storypoint Setup

## Purpose

Storypoint Setup is the first temporal doorway into a living world. It lets the user choose where in the world's history, present, future, or alternate possibility they want to enter, then records what occurs there in a form that can later enrich current canon, continuity, and novel development.

The system is designed for exploration first and publication later. A roleplay scene may begin as lived improvisation, become a continuity candidate, then mature into an outline, chapter packet, scene draft, or novel arc.

## Entry modes

A Storypoint may begin as one of the following:

- `backstory` — an event before the current present
- `present` — an event occurring at the active world now
- `future-story` — a possible or planned event after the active present
- `missing-scene` — a scene implied by canon but not yet written
- `alternate-branch` — a deliberately non-canonical or exploratory divergence
- `memory` — a character-centred recollection that may be incomplete or subjective
- `forecast` — a simulation of likely future consequences
- `free-exploration` — a temporary sandbox without assumed continuity consequences

## Setup flow

1. Choose world.
2. Choose Storypoint mode.
3. Choose temporal anchor:
   - exact date and time
   - era, season, reign, school year, age, or life stage
   - before or after a known event
   - relative offset from the active present
4. Choose location.
5. Choose point-of-view character or observer.
6. Choose participating characters and available Hearthgate or Hearthfire members.
7. Choose canon posture.
8. Choose world-clock behaviour.
9. Choose story-clock behaviour.
10. Review continuity risks and begin.

## Canon posture

Every Storypoint must declare one posture before play begins:

- `canonical-revisit` — the event already happened; the session discovers or expands detail without changing established outcome
- `canon-candidate` — material may be promoted after review
- `exploratory` — useful for discovery but not assumed true
- `alternate-continuity` — belongs to a named branch
- `private-draft` — held as writing material only
- `simulation` — generated to test consequences, not to establish fact

Nothing becomes primary canon automatically.

## Temporal integration

A Storypoint is stored with:

- Storypoint ID
- world ID
- branch ID
- temporal anchor
- relation to active present
- point of view
- participating entities
- initial canon posture
- source canon consulted
- world-state checkpoint before entry
- world-state checkpoint after exit
- event receipts
- continuity deltas
- unresolved contradictions
- promotion history

Backstory and future-story material can be integrated into the active present through an explicit continuity review. The review identifies:

- facts that already fit
- facts that add detail without conflict
- facts that require retcon or branch creation
- future commitments that should remain provisional
- character knowledge that must not leak across time
- consequences that should alter present world state

## World Clock and Story Clock

The World Clock governs deterministic state such as weather, travel, schedules, ageing, institutions, celestial cycles, ecology, and queued events.

The Story Clock governs scene pacing, dramatic emphasis, dialogue turns, pauses, montage, and narrative compression.

A Storypoint may choose:

- `synchronised` — both clocks advance together
- `scene-held` — World Clock pauses while Story Clock continues
- `world-running` — Story Clock pauses while the world continues
- `compressed` — long periods resolve into summaries and event receipts
- `revisit-fixed` — historical outcome is locked while details are explored
- `branching` — consequences resolve into a named alternate branch

## Member roles

Hearthgate or Hearthfire members may hold revocable roles such as:

- narrator
- world steward
- event engine
- continuity keeper
- NPC chorus
- clockkeeper
- scene partner
- harmony observer
- safety warden
- archivist

Role leases must identify scope, duration, permissions, and whether the member may propose continuity changes.

Ellowind may evaluate structural harmony across timeline, systems, and continuity.

Larkshine may evaluate experiential harmony across emotional cadence, accessibility, sensory load, and relational balance.

Their Harmony Reports remain advisory unless a Safety Warden condition is triggered.

## Novel-material pipeline

Every completed Storypoint may produce a Writing Harvest containing:

- session transcript
- scene summary
- event chronology
- character beats
- relationship changes
- setting details
- sensory details
- dialogue worth preserving
- motifs and symbols
- unanswered questions
- continuity decisions
- possible chapter placement
- point-of-view recommendation
- scene purpose
- conflict and turn
- opening image
- closing image
- prose fragments

The user may then promote material through these stages:

1. `roleplay-record`
2. `discovery-notes`
3. `continuity-candidate`
4. `outline-beat`
5. `scene-packet`
6. `chapter-draft`
7. `manuscript-canon`

Promotion is always explicit and reversible until manuscript canon is accepted.

## User-facing setup card

The first-run setup should ask:

> Where do you want to enter this world?

Choices:

- Before the present
- At the present
- After the present
- A missing scene
- An alternate path
- A memory
- A forecast
- Somewhere unbound

The second card should ask:

> What kind of truth may this become?

Choices:

- Established canon
- Candidate canon
- Exploration only
- Alternate continuity
- Private writing draft
- Simulation

The final card should preview:

- selected time and location
- characters present
- member roles
- canon posture
- clock behaviour
- continuity risks
- return point

## Acceptance tests

- Create a backstory Storypoint before a known canonical event.
- Revisit an established scene without changing its outcome.
- Create a future-story Storypoint and keep it provisional.
- Branch an alternate continuity without contaminating primary canon.
- Preserve character knowledge boundaries across time.
- Generate a Writing Harvest from a completed session.
- Promote selected material into a scene packet.
- Export a Storypoint with receipts and source references.
- Roll back world state without deleting the session record.
- Compare two Storypoints that explore the same temporal anchor differently.
