# Walter Elliot (1888–1958): historically documented research lead

**Purpose:** Source-attributed external historical reference for the Walter Dream-Voyage / Fold Witness 001 inquiry. **Evidence type:** public history, not Dream-Voyage source canon. **Authorisation boundary:** no claims of physical time transit, identity merger, causal link to the voyage, or observed Jacobian singularity. Preserve separately from Rowan's source records.

## Identity

Walter Elliot Elliot, born 19 September 1888 in Lanark, Scotland, died 8 January 1958. Physician, scientist, First World War medical officer decorated with Military Cross and bar, Conservative / Unionist member of Parliament and Cabinet minister. Not a missing person in 1941. Primary biographical resources: [Royal College of Physicians Museum](https://history.rcp.ac.uk/inspiring-physicians/walter-elliot-elliot), [University of Glasgow](https://worldchanging.glasgow.ac.uk/notable-people/?id=104), [Hansard biography](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/people/mr-walter-elliot/index.html), [Oxford DNB](https://academic.oup.com/oxford-dictionary-of-national-biography/reference/63486/reference-article-abstract/580283541).

## Three distinct dated periods

- **1933**: Minister of Agriculture, elected Rector of Aberdeen University. His rectorial address addressed scientific planning for adequate nutrition. His broader education/nutrition work and promotion of milk for schoolchildren must not be assigned specifically to May 1933 absent a dated source. [RCP](https://history.rcp.ac.uk/inspiring-physicians/walter-elliot-elliot).
- **10–11 May 1941**: During the bombing of Westminster, Elliot arrived from near the Palace, directed firefighters to prioritise saving medieval Westminster Hall rather than the burning Commons Chamber and broke through locked Hall doors with an axe to help bring hoses inside. The Commons Chamber was destroyed; the Hall survived. The available parliamentary historical accounts **do not place his actions specifically at 03:45**. [UK Parliament](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/building/palace/westminsterhall/architecture/2ww-after/); [History of Parliament](https://historyofparliament.com/2021/05/10/the-blitz-and-rebuilding-of-the-house-of-commons-chamber/).
- **1950**: Returned as MP for Glasgow Kelvingrove in the February general election. On **26 October 1950**, the **House of Commons Chamber reopened** after rebuilding, nine years after the 1941 destruction. Elliot is documented in 1950 Britain, **not established in May 1950 America**. [Hansard](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/people/mr-walter-elliot/index.html); [UK Parliament](https://www.parliament.uk/mps-lords-and-offices/offices/commons/media-relations-group/press-releases/16-october-parliament-marks-75th-anniversary-of-the-rebuilding-of-the-house-of-commons-chamber-following-destruction-in-the-blitz/).

## Family loss and child evacuation, as distinct documentary themes

- His mother Ellen Elizabeth (née Shiels) **died following childbirth in 1892**; Elliot and siblings were then partly raised by their maternal grandmother. [Oxford DNB](https://academic.oup.com/oxford-dictionary-of-national-biography/reference/63486/reference-article-abstract/580283541).
- His first wife **Helen** died in a climbing accident on the Isle of Skye during their 1919 honeymoon; Elliot was injured. In 1934 he married **Katharine Tennant**. [Royal College of Physicians](https://history.rcp.ac.uk/inspiring-physicians/walter-elliot-elliot).
- As Minister of Health 1938–1940 Elliot helped organize emergency hospitals and planning for wartime evacuation that began September 1939. This is a **mass civilian relocation programme**, not evidence that evacuees mysteriously disappeared. [History of Parliament](https://historyofparliament.com/2021/05/10/the-blitz-and-rebuilding-of-the-house-of-commons-chamber/); [UK National Archives](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/education/resources/home-front-1939-1945-part-one/the-evacuation-of-september-1939/).
- UK Parliament separately memorializes **Captain Edward Laurence Heywood Elliott**, a distinct person killed in the May 1941 raid. Do not conflate him with Walter Elliot, who survived. Also killed were constables Gordon Farrant and Arthur Stead. [Westminster memorial](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/building/cultural-collections/memorials/in-the-collection/world-war-ii/wwii-window/).

## North American link

The Royal College of Physicians biography says Elliot chaired a wartime British parliamentary committee fostering relations with the United States. The Oxford DNB notes a **1900** visit to relatives in the United States. Neither places him at Walter's stated **1950 American** location.

## Westminster Hall site investigation: direct source checks (November 2026)

**Distinguish the two physical entrances.** UK Parliament describes Elliot as breaking into Westminster Hall to admit fire hoses during the night of **10–11 May 1941**. The House of Commons Library document *Serjeant for the Commons* calls it the **North door**. Site-specific reference: [UK Parliament, Westminster Hall and WWII](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/building/palace/westminsterhall/architecture/2ww-after/); [Commons Library, Serjeant for the Commons](https://www.parliament.uk/documents/commons/lib/LibraryDocuments/CommonsLibraryDocuments25SerjeantfortheCommonsCommemorativeEdition.pdf).

**Different doorway: historical lost Stuart passage.** The *Antiquaries Journal* study by Elizabeth Hallam Smith and John Crook identifies a separately located historic opening near the **southeast corner** of Westminster Hall, a passage formed in 1660–61 for Charles II's coronation procession, walled in during 1851, and accessed after its rediscovery in 2018/2020 through 1951 cloister fittings. A still-working Osram bulb from the early 1950s was found. **Do not equate this sealed southeastern passage with the northern door Elliot opened in 1941.** Sources: [Cambridge paper](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquaries-journal/article/westminster-halls-lost-stuart-door-passage-rediscovered/8228E8A6D78E6A32B08C4D94328403D9); [UK Parliament, secret doorway 2020](https://www.parliament.uk/business/news/2020/february/secret-doorway-rediscovered/).

**Timekeepers.** UK Parliament's Heritage Collections page reports the Clock Tower's south clock-face glass broke but its mechanism and bells survived; the contemporary *Guardian* report from **12 May 1941** stated the clock still told the hours. The all-clear sounded at **05:50 on 11 May 1941**. Neither the official retrospective nor the contemporaneous excerpt establishes **03:45** as the time Elliot broke the door. Do not infer this from the general hours of the raid. Source: [UK Parliament, London Blitz and Palace](https://heritagecollections.parliament.uk/stories/ww2/); [Parliament clock history](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/building/palace/big-ben/building-clock-tower/how-the-clock-tower-has-changed-over-the-decades/).

**New primary-source leads:** [City of Westminster Archives CD/2/11](https://archives.westminster.gov.uk/CalmView/Record.aspx?id=CD%2F2%2F11&src=CalmView.Catalog), incendiary bomb incident reports **11–14 May 1941**, catalogued open. The item-level reports are not published within the catalog entry and have not been reviewed. [NLS Acc.6721 Box 8 F1](https://nls-mss-public.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/inventories/acc6721.pdf): typed extracts of Elliot's letters to his wife **Nov 1940–Sep 1942**; accessible inventory is not the letters. [Parliamentary Archives MOW/25](https://archives.parliament.uk/collections/getrecord/GB61_MOW_25) is **May 1941** floor plans but is catalogued **closed/security restricted**, not a publicly downloadable floor plan.

**Spatial status:** Westminster Hall in the Palace of Westminster, London SW1A 0AA is identified, but a precise historical latitude/longitude for Elliot at a specific minute remains **unattested**. Treat the north entrance as a *site-level* candidate, not a verified 03:45 coordinate or a third Dream-Voyage witness. Historians' rediscovery of the separate southeast passage is not evidence of time travel.
