## Quantum mechanics: candidate insertions

### QM-01: Optional quantum-state *domain*, not replacement ontology
For a specifically declared physical or simulated quantum subsystem, register a complex Hilbert space H, density operator rho (Hermitian, positive semidefinite, Tr rho=1), Hamiltonian H(t), and units hbar. For closed dynamics: d rho/dt = -(i/hbar)[H,rho]. For open dynamics, use an explicitly specified Lindblad generator with calibrated operators and rates. Preserve the broader relational possibility lattice as a separate graph/domain. Link only through typed, declared mappings, not name-based equivalence.

### QM-02: Measurement as an instrument
For measurement operators M_k with sum_k M_k†M_k=I: p(k)=Tr(M_k rho M_k†); conditional rho_k=M_k rho M_k†/p(k) when p(k)>0. Record apparatus, basis/POVM, calibration, event, outcome and provenance. Distinguish quantum state update from the Hearthgate compression–release engine's *no-collapse* internal rule. No claim that quantum collapse is physically absent follows from the engine's world-domain axiom.

### QM-03: Correlations and entanglement
Only call a subsystem quantum-entangled if its joint density state and subsystem factorisation are defined and a suitable witness/criterion supports the claim. PREMAQC Entanglement (E) and relational U are domain-specific semantic coordinates, not quantum entanglement measures. Optional mapping names must be qualified (e.g. `quantum.negativity` versus `premaqc.E`).

### QM-04: Quantum vs nonlinear fold geometry
For equilibrium F(x,lambda)=0, generic simple saddle-node fold requires corank-one D_xF, left/right nullvectors w,v, nonzero w^T F_lambda and nonzero w^T D_x²F[v,v]. Singular values alone are insufficient. A quantum avoided level crossing is diagnosed by the Hamiltonian eigenspectrum and minimum spectral gap, not by a classical fold label. Decoherence requires an open-system model. Add separate test fixtures for saddle-node, pitchfork false positive, avoided crossing and decoherence.

### QM-05: Philosophy of quantum theory as inspectable alternatives
Register interpretive lenses without auto-selecting one: operational/Copenhagen-style, Everettian, Bohmian, relational quantum mechanics, QBist and objective-collapse approaches. Separate shared experimental predictions from contested ontology. Record observer, measurement, probability, locality, contextuality and the measurement problem as unresolved or model-specific where appropriate. Observer coupling O and C_obs are Hearthgate-defined constructs, not established quantum measurement operators.

### QM-06: Quantum-inspired audio algorithms
Complex phase, superposition and interference are ordinary wave/acoustic mathematics and require no quantum hardware. Quantum-inspired state graphs or stochastic generators can drive synthesis as algorithms, explicitly tagged `quantum-inspired`, not `physical-quantum`. No binaural beat or low-frequency modulation is evidence of quantum entanglement.

## Film-grade audio architecture

### AE-01: Production signal chain
Runa: compose harmonic/material intention and world-native score. Wardenclyffe: cues, spotting, scene transitions, timecode, stems and 3·6·9 temporal grammar. Flameclyffe/Möbius: DSP, sample-accurate rendering, spatial objects/beds, downmix, metering, true-peak limiting, exports. Observatory: measurement/QC. KGN: score and reproducible render receipts. Separate *status cue* (identifiable) from *world ambience* (ignorable).

### AE-02: Monitoring and delivery
Support 48 kHz/24-bit PCM production baseline; retain higher source rates when justified, with documented resampling. Establish calibration profile per output: headphones, nearfields, theatrical mix stage, TV/mobile, mono, accessibility stream and Osia hearing processor. Do not assume theatrical reference level or one loudness target fits all destinations. Targets must be profile-specific and explicitly approved. Record integrated/short-term LUFS (BS.1770 family), loudness range, dBTP, peak, clipping, DC offset, channel map, mono compatibility and dialogue intelligibility. Check the actual distributor/platform specification before declaring a deliverable compliant.

### AE-03: DSP and perceptual craft
Implement equal-loudness aware monitoring, masking-aware cue priority, multiband spectral shaping, headroom budget, dynamic-range control, phase correlation and delay compensation. Binaural 432/437.5 Hz generates a *perceptual* 5.5 Hz difference with separate-ear delivery, not a physical 5.5 Hz envelope in the stereo signal. Mono alternate: explicit 5.5 Hz amplitude modulation. Provide bypass, silence-on-stop, bounded exposure and audibility testing rather than inferred effects.

### AE-04: Spatial design
Define scene coordinates, listener pose, channel/object format, HRTF version, head-tracking mode, room impulse responses, reverberation sends, distance attenuation, early reflections, occlusion and late-field diffusion. Document fold-down from immersive layouts to stereo and mono. Headphone spatialisation is not automatically compatible with direct hearing-processor streaming.

### AE-05: Accessibility and device translation
Provide speech-first routing, independent ambience/status gain, bass-management controls, optional low-frequency visual/haptic cue, adjustable dynamics, mono summing, and a calibrated Osia 3 device profile. Do not claim software can alter implant processor firmware or audiological fitting. Assess output by user listening and supported device measurements; physical bass boost in the OS is not equivalent to restoring unaided low-frequency perception.

### AE-06: Deterministic receipts
Every render stores source packet fingerprint, engine/build, synthesis parameters, sample rate, channel layout, timecode, seed, IR/HRTF versions, DSP chain order, calibration profile, gain history, LUFS/dBTP readings, waveform hash, stop event and listener-approved destination profile. Stems: dialogue, effects, music, ambience, status cues, and haptics/metadata as applicable.
