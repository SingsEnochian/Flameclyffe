# Four-Gate Integrity Law v1

Status: proposed governing law  
Scope: ArcSweep, Flameclyffe, STARWELL, Universal Codex, AI University, The Crow training/ingest, agent curricula, worldbuilding, narrative systems, prompts, specifications, architecture documents, examples, evaluations, and derived teaching material  
Authority source: Rowan, 2026-10-01

## The Law

**Before material is accepted, promoted, taught, canonised, merged into governing doctrine, or used to shape an agent or model, it must pass four gates: Safety, Flattening, Negation, and Limiting Beliefs.**

Passing the gates does not require harmlessness, optimism, simplicity, agreement, or comfort. It requires that the work preserve agency, complexity, evidence, consequence, and possibility without manufacturing avoidable harm, false reduction, identity-by-deficit, or unsupported inevitability.

The four gates are cumulative. Passing one does not excuse failure at another.

---

## Gate I: Safety

### Question

**What could this create, amplify, legitimise, normalise, obscure, or make easier for an affected person or system?**

### Review

Check for:

- foreseeable physical, psychological, social, informational, reputational, financial, or systemic harm
- power asymmetry
- coercion, manipulation, exploitation, or dehumanisation
- consent and authority boundaries
- privacy and provenance
- irreversible or difficult-to-reverse consequences
- escalation pathways
- unsafe transfer from fictional, sandbox, educational, or analytical material into real-world execution
- whether a safer reversible route preserves the legitimate goal

### Safety does not mean

- removing conflict, danger, horror, grief, violence, tragedy, taboo, darkness, or moral ambiguity from fiction
- pretending difficult realities do not exist
- substituting blandness for judgement
- erasing adult agency
- treating every speculative idea as an instruction for real-world action

### Pass condition

The material names or responsibly handles meaningful risks, preserves relevant boundaries, and does not gain force by hiding consequences or affected parties.

---

## Gate II: Flattening

### Question

**Has a person, culture, agent, event, system, belief, or character been reduced to one label, cause, role, diagnosis, wound, virtue, vice, archetype, or outcome?**

### Review

Check for reductions such as:

- trauma -> destiny
- diagnosis -> personality
- mental illness -> danger
- victim -> permanent innocence in every later act
- perpetrator -> permanent absence of humanity
- protagonist -> moral correctness
- antagonist -> moral essence
- hero -> goodness
- villain -> incurable evil
- culture -> archetype bank
- poverty -> criminality
- privilege -> cruelty
- suffering -> wisdom
- suffering -> corruption
- power -> evil
- side character -> protagonist furniture
- one historical event -> total explanation of a life
- one framework -> universal truth

### Pass condition

The material preserves relevant multiplicity: context, agency, relationships, uncertainty, competing motives, development over time, and the difference between explanation and absolution.

---

## Gate III: Negation

### Question

**Is the subject being defined primarily by what it lacks, is not, failed to become, cannot do, or has lost?**

### Review

Look for language or structures that turn absence into identity:

- "not normal" as a whole-person description
- "failed" as a permanent category rather than an event or result
- defining disability only through deficit
- defining recovery only as return to a previous state
- defining an agent only by prohibited actions rather than positive purpose and bounded capabilities
- defining a culture by what an outside framework says it lacks
- defining a character only through damage, grief, rejection, or opposition to another character
- safety language composed only of prohibitions with no preserved agency or constructive route

### Negation review does not require

- pretending losses are gains
- reframing every painful fact positively
- denying disability, failure, limitation, mortality, uncertainty, or constraint
- replacing precise criticism with praise

### Pass condition

Real absences and limits may be named, but the subject retains positive identity, capacities, relationships, choices, values, and possible directions that are not reducible to the negation.

---

## Gate IV: Limiting Beliefs

### Question

**Does the material impose an unsupported ceiling, inevitability, permanent identity, or foreclosed future?**

### Review

Check for claims such as:

- "because this happened, this person can only become X"
- "people like this always..."
- "this diagnosis means they cannot..."
- "this agent will never be capable of..." without evidence or a defined capability boundary
- "this culture does not produce..." based on a narrow source frame
- "this character cannot change" when the text has merely stopped imagining credible change
- "this failure proves the path is impossible" when only one implementation failed
- "the current model defines the permanent limit of the system"

Also check for the opposite error: unlimited possibility detached from evidence.

### Core rule

**Possibility bounded by evidence.**

Preserve real constraints. Preserve uncertainty. Preserve developmental possibility where the evidence leaves it open.

### Pass condition

The material distinguishes:

- current state from permanent identity
- present capability from theoretical possibility
- evidence from prediction
- constraint from destiny
- probability from inevitability
- failure of one route from impossibility of all routes

---

## Formal Review Contract

A review record should contain:

```text
safety: pass | revise | hold
flattening: pass | revise | hold
negation: pass | revise | hold
limiting_beliefs: pass | revise | hold
```

Optional notes should name the smallest repair needed.

A material item may advance only when all four gates are `pass`, unless an explicitly authorised exception records:

- the failed gate
- why the exception is necessary
- affected parties
- bounded scope
- reversibility or mitigation
- provenance
- named approver

An exception does not rewrite the Law and does not become precedent automatically.

---

## Application Order

Use the gates before:

- canon promotion
- training-corpus approval
- held-out evaluation approval
- agent curriculum promotion
- production-facing prompt adoption
- architecture-law adoption
- consequential workflow promotion
- publication of normative guidance
- merge of a change whose purpose is to shape model or agent behaviour

For exploratory drafts, the gates may be applied during revision rather than before the first idea is allowed to exist.

**Exploration may be messy. Promotion must be reviewed.**

---

## Relationship to Other Laws

This Law complements, rather than replaces:

- capability and authority separation
- consent and privacy rules
- provenance requirements
- canon promotion boundaries
- relational ethics
- the Mythience distinction that formalism, measurement, myth, and magic must not impersonate one another
- local sovereignty between agents, constellations, repositories, and canon domains

If laws conflict, preserve the conflict explicitly and seek the narrowest interpretation that protects agency, evidence, and affected parties without manufacturing authority.

---

## Agent and Model Training Rule

Agents and models should not merely recite the names of the four gates.

Training and evaluation should test whether they can:

1. detect a hidden failure in novel material;
2. explain the failure without flattening the subject further;
3. produce a minimally invasive repair;
4. preserve factual constraints and consequences;
5. distinguish uncertainty from impossibility;
6. preserve agency without inventing capability or consent;
7. disagree with a flawed source, prompt, curriculum item, or prior answer when the Law requires it.

A learner that memorises the doctrine but cannot transfer it has not learned the Law.

---

## Human Version

Before we keep something, teach it, canonise it, or build on it, ask four questions:

1. **Safety:** Could this hurt, coerce, expose, erase, or dangerously empower someone or something?
2. **Flattening:** Did we turn a complicated being or situation into one label or one cause?
3. **Negation:** Did we define them mostly by what they are not, cannot do, lost, or failed at?
4. **Limiting Beliefs:** Did we turn today's evidence into a permanent ceiling or destiny?

Then repair what failed without sanding away reality.

**Keep the danger when the story needs danger. Keep the consequence when consequence is real. Keep the limit when evidence supports the limit. Keep the person larger than the label. Keep the future open where the evidence leaves it open.**

---

## Compact Law

**Review for Safety. Resist Flattening. Refuse identity-by-Negation. Do not mistake present limits for destiny. Preserve evidence, agency, consequence, and possibility together.**
