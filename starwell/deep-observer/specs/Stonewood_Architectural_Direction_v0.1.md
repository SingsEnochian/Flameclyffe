# Stonewood Architectural Direction v0.1

## Purpose

This spec defines the architectural direction for Stonewood cities in Terra Aeterna.

The direction blends Norse structure, Japanese spatial/ritual design, and a unique Terra Aeterna organic neo-futurism where buildings look grown, shaped, sung, or coaxed into form rather than mechanically assembled.

## Canon sentence

```text
Stonewood architecture is Norse-rooted, Japanese-thresholded, and Terra Aeterna-grown: organic neo-futurism that feels cultivated by ritual engineering rather than built by machines alone.
```

## Design pillars

### 1. Norse root

Use Norse influence for:

- structural severity
- timber/stone memory
- roof weight
- hall logic
- runic boundary language
- carved beams
- hearth-like civic centres
- long axial gathering halls
- weather-respecting forms
- ritual thresholds

Avoid making it look like generic fantasy Viking cosplay.

### 2. Japanese threshold

Use Japanese influence for:

- torii-like gates
- shrine paths
- layered roofs
- careful thresholds
- gardens and negative space
- raised walkways
- screens/lattices
- courtyards
- path sequencing
- water, stone, moss, lantern relationships
- asymmetry held in balance

Avoid simply pasting torii gates onto unrelated buildings.

### 3. Terra Aeterna growth

Use Terra Aeterna influence for:

- grown civic material
- stonewood grain
- luminous seams
- root-like structural ribs
- buildings that flow from terrain
- living observatory forms
- soft futurism
- ritual engineering
- field-responsive architecture
- organic infrastructure
- city-planning as a living system

Avoid sterile sci-fi blocks or arbitrary biotech goo.

## Material language

Stonewood should feel like:

- pale carved stone
- moon-bleached wood
- milk-white living mineral
- warm bone-gold underlayers
- faint silver/blue glow channels
- moss and lichen integration
- black glass or obsidian accent panels
- brushed copper / aged iron fittings
- soft lantern glass
- root-vein inlays

## Form language

Use:

- sweeping rooflines that feel grown into the wind
- long hall silhouettes softened by organic ribs
- domes that look seed-pod or shell-like
- towers like tree trunks, shrine pillars, or observatory spines
- torii-like gate frames integrated into civic routes
- branchlike bridges
- terrace layers following terrain
- rounded plazas like tree rings or water ripples
- carved rune/lattice edgework
- stonewood arches that appear coaxed upward

Avoid:

- square corporate glass towers
- random neon
- hard cyberpunk clutter
- generic medieval blocks
- unsupported fantasy spikes
- mismatched Viking/Japanese collage

## City planning principles

Stonewood cities should be readable as planned ecosystems.

### Core structure

Each city should have:

- civic heart / hearth centre
- primary axis or ring
- shrine/threshold route
- market/social node
- observatory or engine site
- water or grove relationship
- residential terraces or districts
- transit/road logic
- public squares
- quiet paths
- service routes
- gates and edges

### Growth logic

The city should look as though it grew from:

- coastline
- cliff
- grove
- river bend
- mountain saddle
- crater/ring
- old ruin
- engine resonance field
- threshold site

Architecture should respond to terrain.

## Roads and paths

Roads and paths must be designed, not sprinkled.

### Main roads

Main roads should follow civic logic:

- harbour to market
- gate to civic heart
- civic heart to observatory
- shrine route to threshold
- residential terraces to public squares
- ridge to watchtower
- grove to healing district

### Path feel

Stonewood paths should feel:

- walkable
- ceremonial when needed
- softly lit
- integrated with water/groves/terraces
- sometimes stair-like
- sometimes bridge-like
- sometimes grown from root-ribs

### Path materials

Use:

- pale stonewood slabs
- root-inlaid seams
- lantern studs
- moss-edge borders
- dark stone drainage grooves
- carved directional runes
- quiet copper rails
- water-channel companions

## Infrastructure

Stonewood infrastructure should be beautiful but functional.

### Light network

- lantern roads
- gate beacons
- soft path studs
- tower glows
- moon-glass panels
- emergency amber/red warning runes

### Water network

- canals
- rain chains
- cistern gardens
- reflective pools
- purification stairs
- mist channels

### Sound/resonance network

- bell towers
- resonance pylons
- Wardenclyffe-like field structures
- hum channels
- quiet zones
- tonal wayfinding

### Transit network

- pedestrian primary
- bridge corridors
- small carts or gliding civic vehicles if needed
- canal routes
- lift stairs / platform terraces
- ritual paths not used for ordinary traffic

## Palette

Base palette:

- milk-white stonewood
- pale ash
- moon silver
- sea-glass blue
- moss green
- deep pine
- black glass
- aged copper

- storm grey

Accent palette by field mood:

- quiet/cool: blue-white, silver, pale teal

## Procreate art direction

When drawing Stonewood cities:

- start with terrain silhouette
- place civic heart
- define main axis/ring
- add gates/harbour/observatory
- place district masses
- connect with roads/paths
- add architectural ribs
- add lantern/light network
- add organic details last

Do not begin with ornament. Begin with city logic.

## Public-safe description

Stonewood cities are grown civic organisms: Norse in their rooted strength, Japanese in their threshold discipline, and Terra Aeterna in their living organic futurism. Their roads, gates, lanterns, and observatories are not decoration; they are how the city thinks.

## Withness note

A Stonewood city should feel old enough to remember storms, alive enough to answer light, and planned enough that someone could find the market without needing divine intervention.
