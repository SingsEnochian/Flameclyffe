# Elara Vocal Duet Architecture

## Canonical Vocal Pair

The primary vocal elements are a duet:

- First soprano
- Baritone

They are two distinct narrative voices, not a lead singer with a backing shadow and not a generic blended choir.

## Dramatic Function

### First Soprano

Carries:

- illumination
- invitation
- transformed memory
- questions that open the harmonic field
- the upward or outward arc
- the luminous crown of a phrase

Default character:

- clear rather than piercing
- warm rather than glassy
- expressive without excessive vibrato
- capable of intimacy at low dynamics
- never forced into a permanently high tessitura

Preferred comfortable range for the current work:

- A3 to E5 for most writing
- F-sharp5 to B5 reserved for brief structural peaks
- no exposed sustained pitch above B5

### Baritone

Carries:

- memory
- grounding
- embodied answer
- protection without domination
- the downward or inward arc
- the weight that allows the soprano line to rise safely

Default character:

- resonant but not booming
- intimate rather than theatrical
- articulate in the middle register
- capable of tenderness, uncertainty, and restraint
- never reduced to a continuous low drone

Preferred comfortable range for the current work:

- A2 to E4 for most writing
- F-sharp4 to G4 reserved for brief emotional peaks
- avoid prolonged writing below B2 or above F-sharp4

## Duet Relationship

The duet must behave as a relationship between two independent musical intelligences.

Use:

- call and response
- exchanged motifs
- contrary motion
- overlapping entries
- thirds and sixths for intimacy
- open fifths for foundation
- suspensions that resolve through listening
- shared notes at thresholds
- occasional crossed contour without forcing crossed tessitura

Avoid:

- both voices singing every line together
- constant octave doubling
- soprano permanently above baritone in identical rhythm
- generic cinematic vowel pads replacing actual duet writing
- automatic power-ballad belting
- choral stacking that erases the two identities

## Structural Use Across the Four Movements

### Movement I · The Abyss Foundation

- Baritone enters first, close and restrained.
- Soprano appears as a distant answering interval, not immediately as a full melody.
- Their first shared harmony should be an open fifth or a quiet minor third.
- The baritone carries the tonic memory while the soprano introduces the first transformed contour.

### Movement II · The Silver Horizon

- The voices begin exchanging complete phrases.
- Use flowing sixths, suspensions, and contrary motion.
- The soprano may lead the Arc material while the baritone carries Bridge and Anchor functions.
- Stereo placement may widen slightly, but the duet must still feel relational rather than separated.

### Movement III · The Solar Surge

- Rhythmic dialogue becomes more active.
- The baritone may initiate propulsion rather than merely support it.
- The soprano may answer with ascending sequence or luminous extension.
- Use brief unisons as declarations, then split again into independent lines.

### Movement IV · The Full Spiral Return

- Both voices carry transformed versions of motifs introduced earlier.
- Counterpoint becomes fully integrated with the five instrumental layers.
- The emotional summit may use a brief unison or octave, but only after the duet has earned it through prior independence.
- The final cadence should resolve through shared breath, not an endlessly sustained top note.

## Phrase Rules

- Each vocal phrase must have a singable contour.
- Text setting must respect natural stress and vowel shape.
- Final syllables should generally release within 0.75 to 1.25 beats unless a longer suspension has a clear harmonic purpose.
- Avoid repeated hard consonant attacks at every chapter boundary.
- Use staggered breathing so the duet can remain continuous without sounding mechanically looped.
- A held note must contain harmonic, dynamic, or timbral development.

## Wordless and Texted Modes

The duet may operate in either mode:

### Wordless Vocalise

Use open, comfortable vowels such as ah, oh, oo, eh, and humming consonants sparingly.

The vowel must follow register:

- warmer, rounder vowels for high soprano writing
- clearer central vowels for baritone articulation
- avoid bright exposed ee on high sustained notes

### Texted Duet

Use lyrics when the narrative chapter provides language worth carrying musically.

The voices may:

- sing different but related lines
- complete one another's sentences
- repeat a transformed phrase from the other voice
- converge on a shared word at a structural threshold

Do not force every canonical sentence into lyrics. Select language for musical necessity.

## Five-Layer Relationship

The soprano and baritone are not counted as replacements for the five instrumental-harmonic layers.

The full arrangement contains:

1. Foundation
2. Inner Motion
3. Narrative Instrumental Voice
4. Luminous Colour
5. Crown
6. First Soprano
7. Baritone

The instrumental layers support, answer, and transform the duet. They must leave spectral and rhythmic space for intelligible voices.

## Mix and Spectral Rules

- Keep the soprano's strongest energy below the active B5 ceiling.
- Use de-essing and gentle high-shelf control rather than dulling the entire voice.
- Keep the baritone clear around the low-mid range; avoid masking with cello, horn, or synth bass.
- Do not centre both voices at identical loudness throughout.
- Permit subtle spatial distinction, but preserve a coherent shared acoustic world.
- The Twist must remain quieter than either singer at their softest meaningful entry.

## Suno Handoff Language

Describe the vocal pair as:

"A true duet between a warm first soprano and an intimate resonant baritone. Independent melodic lines, call and response, contrary motion, thirds and sixths, occasional earned unison, restrained vibrato, no belting, no generic choir, no piercing high notes, and no endless sustained final syllables."

Do not request imitation of a specific living singer.

## Audition Requirement

Before rendering the complete song, produce a 30 to 60 second duet audition containing:

- one baritone-led phrase
- one soprano answer
- one overlap in contrary motion
- one brief shared consonance
- one short release into the next instrumental phrase

The audition passes only when both voices remain individually recognisable and the listener can hear the relationship without reading an explanation.
