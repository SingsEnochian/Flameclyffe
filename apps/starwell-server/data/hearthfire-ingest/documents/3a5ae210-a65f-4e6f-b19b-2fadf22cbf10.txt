# Lantern HUD — collaborator context & co-design

**Status:** working notes. Captures who Lantern HUD is being designed *with* and *for*, the
collaborator's stated priorities, and the co-design posture (guideline G8). This is durable
context for every Lantern PRD — read it before writing one.

## Who

- **Peter (this repo's owner)** — eye-tracking UI expertise from **Eyefluence** (the gaze-
  interaction company later acquired by Google). Doubly relevant: (a) deep eye-tracking UX
  knowledge — accidental-activation avoidance, dwell alternatives, calibration realities; (b)
  **FTO/patent awareness** — the PMEyeUI FTO bundle (A1+B8+C2+E1–E4) exists precisely to avoid
  Eyefluence-style gaze-interaction infringement, so Peter's input also guards patent posture.
- **The collaborator** — originator of the "Lantern HUD" concept (the founding notes). Early-
  stage, privacy- and scope-careful, building on Maverick. Reached out to Peter specifically for
  the gaze-first accessibility layer. Given the first-person framing of the founding notes
  (night/fatigue/pain-flare scenarios), the collaborator likely has **relevant lived experience**
  — making them both a **domain partner and the co-design tester** (the "one potential tester"
  on record). This is the G8 "nothing about us without us" partner.

## The collaborator's framing (their words, 2026-06-09)

> "My project is **less 'eye tracking as a cursor' and more a fatigue-aware interaction system
> for hands-reduced use**: live captions, gaze-controlled repeat/pause/enlarge/pin/clear actions,
> **basic environment controls**, and eventually **caregiver/safety flows for situations where
> voice or hands may not be reliable**."

What this fixed in the design:
- **Confirms 7.4 Low-Effort Control is the substrate and is written FIRST** — the whole project
  is the fatigue-aware layer; captions and the rest hang off it.
- **Added scope:** *basic environment controls* (lights / simple IoT) as a first real-action
  surface of the layer (goals-and-guidelines §7.4).
- **Caregiver/safety flows** for when voice/hands fail — in scope **only** as wearer-*initiated*
  help, never caregiver *monitoring* (red line R3).

## What the collaborator explicitly asked the PRD to cover

They want Peter's eye-tracking experience to "save them from reinventing several cursed wheels."
Each Lantern PRD therefore carries these required sections (mirrored in goals-and-guidelines §7):

1. **Eye-tracking UI patterns** (Eyefluence-informed) — and the FTO/patent-posture check.
2. **Accidental-activation avoidance** — defeating Midas-touch / unintended commits.
3. **Calibration assumptions** — assumed accuracy + graceful degradation when it breaks.
4. **Dwell alternatives** — non-dwell path for every action; dwell only if reversible + counting-down.
5. **Fatigue / energy budget** — resting cost, gaze-rest, resumability, interaction-cost proxies.
6. **What should wait until real HMD testing** — items the simulator can't honestly validate
   (we are simulator-only — `project_simulator_only`), deferred to hardware + real-tester testing.

## Co-design posture

- The PRD is partly **written for the collaborator to read and co-author** — keep it self-
  contained, jargon-light where possible, and honest about what's speculative vs grounded.
- **Privacy & scope discipline are shared values** — bias to on-device, wearer-owned, minimal
  scope (G6); flag anything that expands data capture.
- Co-design is an **active gate**, not a pending checkbox: open design questions get the
  collaborator's input before they're locked, and lived-experience feedback outranks researcher
  assumptions (G8).
- **What waits for the collaborator/hardware:** anything whose value is the *felt* experience of
  low-effort, non-exhausting interaction — that cannot be settled from the simulator and is
  explicitly deferred to real testing with the collaborator on hardware.
