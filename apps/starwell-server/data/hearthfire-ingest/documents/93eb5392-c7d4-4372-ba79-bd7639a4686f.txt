# Concordance Lens — PRD (the LATER contextual-overlay + on-request recognition layer)

**Status:** ASPIRATIONAL — LATER layer, design only, no code. **Post-MVP.** Concordance Lens
is the **fourth and last** piece of the Lantern Stack and is **explicitly NOT required for
MVP** (Catherine's spec §1, §14; `goals-and-guidelines.md` §7.D). It is designed only after
the MVP trio — **Caption Lantern → Voice Lantern → Sentinel Lantern** — and the **Lantern
Substrate** that all four ride on are real. Nothing here is implemented; every capability
below is a design intent, not a shipped behaviour (repeated for `tools/claims-audit`).

> **Why LATER, restated for every reader:** this layer carries the highest-risk, lowest-
> validated, most-hardware-gated features in the whole suite (on-request scene/text/object
> recognition on a *slow, AI-only camera*; cognitive/memory + neurodivergent overlays whose
> value is *felt*, not screenshot-able; governed emotion/affect recognition that the autistic
> community contests). It must not block, distract from, or destabilise the MVP. It is written
> now so the design is coherent and so the substrate it needs is shaped correctly — **not** so
> it ships next.

## 2. Purpose, target users, scope, non-goals

### 2.1 Purpose

A **wearer-summoned, on-request contextual layer** that, when the wearer asks, helps them
**understand what is in front of them** and **remember the things that fall out of their
head** — reading environment text, identifying objects, summarising a sign / paper / screen,
answering "what am I looking at?", a focus-aware AI query, gentle just-in-time prompts when a
routine stalls, a private name/face recall card, an ambient-time arc for time-blindness, and
(for low-vision) dark-scene edge outlining + a peripheral "look-here" arrow — all on a
device that does **on-request snapshot recognition, not real-time AR** (§4.1, `goals` §1).

The spine, inherited from `research/05` and the calm-tech + autistic-autonomy critiques:
**silence is the default; restraint is in WHEN/IF you show, not dimness (G2); the wearer owns
the config and the data; never coach, never monitor for a third party (R3/R4).**

### 2.2 Target users

Three overlapping cohorts (most users live in more than one — `goals` §2):

- **Cognitive / memory** — TBI, MCI / early dementia, aphasia (word-finding), ADHD (executive
  function, time-blindness, "out of sight = out of mind" / object-permanence). Needs:
  just-in-time prompts; <2 s frictionless capture; context-gated re-surfacing; a memory aid
  that *reduces* dependence (landmark learning, wearer-owned recall), never creates it (R3).
- **Neurodivergent** — autistic and ADHD users. Needs: **predictability and control** above
  all (unexpected pop-ups are sensory aggressors — `05` §5); wearer-summoned > pushed; a hard
  overlay budget + quiet-mode; emotion/affect support governed against the autistic-autonomy
  critique (§5, R4).
- **Low-vision / field-loss / night-blind** — AMD, RP/Usher, glaucoma. Needs (additive-optic-
  honest, `03`): **dark-scene-only** edge/hazard outlining (washes out in daylight); a
  peripheral-field directional "look-here" arrow for field loss; on-request snapshot text/scene
  reading. Never a mobility-aid replacement (R2); audio/haptic is the primary hazard channel,
  the HUD is a glanceable supplement (`03` §3).

### 2.3 Scope (v-later)

From Catherine's spec §14 (verbatim feature set): read environment text; identify objects;
summarise signs / papers / screens; gentle visual prompts; "what am I looking at?"; focus-
aware AI queries. Plus the mined research features (§4). All under her constraints (§7).

### 2.4 Non-goals

- **Not real-time AR.** No continuous world-anchored labels, no live object/face overlay, no
  gaze-steered audio (the small FOV + slow AI-only camera make this impossible on this device —
  `goals` §1, `03` §6). On-request snapshot only.
- **Not always-on capture.** No always-on camera; capture is an explicit per-instance command
  with a clear capture indicator (spec §3, §14, §15; R5).
- **Not cloud-by-default.** No cloud vision by default; on-device-first; cloud is explicit,
  minimal, visible, revocable (G6, spec §5.5, §15).
- **Not stored.** No image storage by default; frames are analysed and discarded unless the
  wearer explicitly saves (spec §14, §15; R5).
- **Not a coach, not a monitor, not a medical/mobility device.** Emotion/affect is a private
  wearer-only reference, never corrective (R4); no caregiver dashboard / no surveillance (R3);
  not a cane/guide-dog replacement (R2); not a diagnosis or vision/hearing-restoration claim (R1).
- **Not a Lantern Stack dependency.** No MVP app depends on Concordance Lens; it is additive.

## 3. The "calm by absence" overlay model (G2)

On this additive optic, **calm cannot mean dim** — dark = transparent, only bright low-
saturation marks are legible (`05` §4, `03` §6). So Concordance Lens implements calm as a
property of **WHEN / IF** it shows, never of how dimly it draws:

- **Default state of every Concordance surface is nothing on screen.** At rest the layer
  contributes **zero** drawables (and zero per-frame cost — project zero-alloc law). The Status
  Bead (owned by the substrate) is the only persistent chrome; Concordance does not add its own.
- **Wearer-summoned, not pushed (autonomy + anti-sensory-overload — `05` §5, R4).** The primary
  interaction is **pull**: an eye-summon / single gesture / voice ("what is this?", "what's
  next?", "who is this?") brings a card up; it is read in a glance and **clears**. Unexpected
  central-FOV pop-ups are sensory aggressors for autistic users — Concordance does **not** push.
- **The COACH show-only-when-stalled pattern (the one inferred surface — `05` §2, COACH/
  Mihailidis).** The just-in-time step prompter is the *single* feature allowed to appear
  without an explicit pull, and **only** when the system infers a stall (dwell / hesitation /
  GPS-or-time context). It surfaces the next step of a wearer-configured routine **one step at
  a time, then clears.** This is the cleanest way to be calm on an optic that can't whisper:
  silence is the default; a cue appears only on inferred difficulty. Any such inferred push is
  **wearer-pre-approved, fades in (never jumps), and is fully mutable in advance** so it is
  *predictable* (`05` §5; adaptive-MR-notifications).
- **The global overlay budget + quiet-mode are owned by the substrate** (`goals` §G2;
  `app-prd-lantern-substrate.md`). Concordance **spends from** that budget; it does not define
  its own. A hard cap on simultaneous elements + a global "mute everything but the essential"
  gesture apply to every Concordance overlay. Treating overlay frequency / dwell-time as a
  *budget the system spends sparingly* is the device-level enforcement of "calm = restraint."
- **Ambient-time arc — the one earned always-present element.** A shrinking peripheral arc /
  remaining-time mark (Tiimo / Time-Timer style) at a **fixed, expected** peripheral position,
  read in one glance, for time-blindness. It earns its persistence precisely because it is
  *predictable and single-glance* (object-permanence for time — `05` §4 design-implication 5).
  The wearer sets where/when it shows; it is opt-in and counts against the overlay budget.

## 4. Feature set, mapped to Risk Levels + device reality

**Honest device reality (load-bearing — `goals` §1, `03` §6, `05` §6).** The Maverick is a
small-FOV (~22°), **additive** optic with a **slow, AI-only camera** (not a real-time CV feed):

- Recognition is **on-request snapshot**: "look → ask → wait for the AI → glanceable result,"
  not continuous AR. Expect seconds, not frames; show a working/queued state, never block.
- The additive optic **ADDS light only — it cannot dim, darken, occlude, or replace** the real
  world. **Overlays must never hide hazards or faces (G4).** Edge outlining works **only in
  dark scenes** and must auto-disable in bright light (Hwang & Peli — `03` §2); the "look-here"
  arrow **cues attention at the edge** rather than redrawing the scene.
- Everything here is **L0 passive HUD** or **L1 local** except the **capture/analysis** action
  (an explicit, indicated camera capture + analysis), which is **L1–L2**. **Nothing in this
  layer is L3+** (no room control, no caregiver/aid, no emergency — those live in Sentinel).

Risk Levels are **declared here, enforced by the substrate's Safety Gate** (`app-prd-lantern-
substrate.md` §3.3). The substrate supplies redundant input (G1), Confirm/Cancel/Undo, the
overlay budget, and Low-Energy / posture modes; this PRD only declares nouns + intents.

| # | Feature (source) | What it does | Risk | Device-reality note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | **"What am I looking at?" / read text / identify object / summarise sign-paper-screen** (spec §14) | Wearer summons → explicit capture (indicated) → on-device-first recognition → one glanceable card (text read / object name / short summary) → clears | **L1** focus+summon; **L2** if it touches a persistent setting or an explicit cloud call | On-request snapshot, seconds-latency, working-state shown; result is advisory, accuracy-honest (show uncertainty) |

| F3 | **Just-in-time stall prompter — COACH** (`05` §2 design-impl 1) | Next step of a wearer-configured routine, shown **only on inferred stall**, one step, then clears | **L0** passive HUD (shows; commits nothing) | The only inferred (non-pull) surface; wearer-pre-approved + mutable; fades in |
| F4 | **Wearer-summoned glanceable cards** (`05` §5 design-impl 2) | Pull-based cue layer ("what's next?"); fades in, single glance, clears | **L0** | No surprise push; predictability/control (R4) |

exposes the required full-screen affordances (reveal + Back + Home + BottomMenu) via the
shared widgets; the **capture indicator is visible whenever the camera is active** (R5,
spec §15); colours clear the additive-optic luminance floor (G2).

## 5. Emotion / affect recognition — governance (R4) and the autistic-autonomy critique

Emotion/affect recognition is **RETAINED but governed**, and is the **highest-risk feature in
the entire Lantern Stack** (`05` §6 goblin 2). It is designed **head-on against** the autistic-
autonomy critique, not in spite of it.

### 5.1 The critique we are designing against (cite, don't footnote — `05` §3)

- **ASAN / "nothing about us without us."** Autism is natural human variation, **not something
  to be fixed**; interventions aimed at making someone "less autistic" are opposed; disabled
  people are the experts in their own experience and must co-design (`05` §3; ASAN, Williams &
  Gilbert). Emotion-recognition glasses framed as a *therapy that coaches a child toward
  normative behaviour*, plus a **caregiver dashboard that monitors "progress,"** read to many
  self-advocates as **surveillance + a compliance project, not support.**
- **Weak / temporary evidence + wrong target.** The Stanford "Superpower Glass" RCT showed
  **small, temporary effects** that had disappeared at 6-week follow-up; recent work suggests
  autistic adults have **precise emotion representations** — the premise that they need to be
  "taught" emotions by an emoji may target the wrong problem (`05` §3).
- **Emotion-AI is inaccurate in the wild.** Classifiers lose ~half their accuracy under
  occlusion and routinely confuse disgust/sadness/anger — a glasses overlay that confidently
  labels a face "angry" may simply be **wrong**, and the wearer pays the social cost (`05` §3).

### 5.2 What we ship instead (R4 design constraints — all mandatory)

Per `goals` §R4, emotion/affect is **broadened beyond autism** to a **general wearer-only
assist** (social/meeting context; users who can't see faces well) and is **never scoped or
marketed as "fixing" autistic people.** Whenever it is built, **all** of the following hold:

- **Default OFF.** Ships disabled; the wearer must explicitly enable it. It is a contested
  feature; contested features default off, wearer-owned (R4, G8).
- **On-request snapshot only.** No always-on affect read — the slow AI-only camera can't do
  live affect anyway (`goals` §1). The wearer summons it for a single frame; a clear capture
  indicator is shown (R5).
- **Wearer-owned output; output goes ONLY to the wearer.** Never broadcast, never logged to a
  third party, never a caregiver feed (R3). The wearer is always the sender and owner.
- **Never a coach.** It is a **private optional reference**, not a corrective instructor; it
  does **not** tell the wearer how to behave or "improve."
- **Accuracy-honest.** It **shows uncertainty and never asserts** an emotion ("possibly
  uncertain" / a hedge, never "angry"). Confidence is rendered; low confidence = no claim.
- **Co-designed, configurable.** Built **with** the communities served, not for them; the
  wearer decides whether it exists at all and what it surfaces (G8).
- **Better default = predictability & control.** Per `05` §3, what autistic users actually ask
  for is predictability and control (the §3 calm model, the overlay budget, wearer-summoned
  cards) — those are the first-class features; emotion-labelling of other humans is the
  last-resort, opt-in, governed edge.

## 6. Collaborator-required sections

### 6.1 Accidental-activation avoidance (Midas-touch defense)

Owned by the substrate (`app-prd-lantern-substrate.md` §6.1) and inherited here; Concordance
specifics:

- **Gaze never consents** (substrate §3.4). A fixation — accidental, curious, or just *reading*
  a sign — must never fire a capture or an AI query. Capture/analysis (F1/F2/F11) requires an
  **explicit second signal** (the substrate's CONFIRM) and shows the capture indicator; gaze
  alone only focuses/previews. This matters acutely here: the wearer's eyes are *on the world*,
  so "looking at a face/sign" is the resting state, not an activation.
- **No inferred capture.** Recognition never auto-fires from gaze dwell on a person or text.
  The only inferred surface is F3 (COACH stall prompt), which is **L0** and **commits nothing**.
- **Capture is L1–L2, undoable / discarded.** A mistaken capture is discarded (no storage by
  default — R5); the universal undo snackbar (substrate §5.1) backs the reversible cases.
- **Quiet-mode + overlay budget** prevent a runaway of cards even if the wearer over-summons.

### 6.2 Calibration assumptions (assumed accuracy + graceful degradation, G9)

- **Assumed accuracy:** the project per-config interactive floor (**2.8° or 3.2° in every
  axis**, config-dependent); Low-Energy mode raises toward 3.2°. **Assumptions-to-verify on
  hardware** (§6.5).
- **This cohort breaks gaze calibration the most** (`03` §6 goblin 5): nystagmus, strabismus,
  and AMD/low-vision **eccentric preferred retinal loci** all defeat standard fixation-based
  calibration. So Concordance must degrade **especially** gracefully — every feature reachable
  via the substrate G1 non-gaze paths (voice / head / switch / phone); a calibration-free dwell
  mode; and the layer must remain useful with **no gaze input at all** (voice "what is this?" +
  a head/switch confirm is a complete path). This is also what keeps the non-ET Raven port
  usable (#330).
- **Pursuit/Orbit preferred** over per-target dwell where it lowers effort and tolerates drift.

### 6.3 Dwell alternatives (non-dwell path for every action)

- **Every action has a non-dwell path** via the G1 binding (tap / head-nod / voice / switch /
  phone). Voice is the natural primary for this layer ("what is this?", "who is this?",
  "what's next?") — it removes the eyes-on-target requirement entirely.
- **Dwell only on reversible (L0/L1) surfaces, with a visible counting-down affordance**
  (~600 ms target, TBD). Capture/analysis is reachable without any dwell. No L3+ here, so no
  dwell-on-consequential-action question arises.

### 6.4 Fatigue / energy budget — especially **cognitive** load

This is the section that matters most for *this* layer: **an overlay that ADDS cognitive load
defeats the entire purpose** (`05` §6 goblin 1; AR demonstrably *increases* load and can cause
inattentional blindness). Rules:

- **The split-attention test for every element.** A Concordance overlay is justified **only if
  it co-locates info with the task and REDUCES total effort** — not if it merely adds a thing to
  the visual field. Every feature in §4 is screened against this; anything that fails is cut.
- **Resting cost ~zero.** Default = nothing on screen (§3); no per-frame rebuild/alloc.
- **Cognitive-load proxies are first-class test metrics** (extending the substrate's interaction-
  cost proxies, substrate §6.4): **elements-on-screen-at-once** (must stay within the overlay
  budget), **time-to-clear** (a summoned card is read and gone in a single glance), and
  **acts-per-task** for capture+result. A fatigue/cognitive layer's value is **not** captured by
  a screenshot — these proxies are.
- **Gaze-rest paths + resumability** inherited from the substrate (Low-Energy mode, non-gaze
  fallbacks, timeout-cancels-never-commits). A capture/query interrupted by brain-fog/PEM is
  resumable; a slow recognition never blocks and is cancellable.
- **Dementia learnability + dignity (`05` §6 goblins 3,4).** Extreme simplicity, consistency,
  zero hidden gestures, audio support, and **support-side (caregiver) configuration so the
  wearer never has to learn an interface** — configuration is support, never monitoring (R3).

### 6.5 What waits for real HMD testing (simulator-only — `project_simulator_only`)

The simulator faithfully validates *logic, geometry, dual-sink parity, the gate state machine,
and the overlay budget*. It **cannot** honestly validate — and this layer is **unusually**
hardware/model-gated:

- **The slow AI-only camera itself** — capture latency, frame quality, and the felt experience
  of "look → ask → wait" are device-gated. The sim can stub a recognition adapter but cannot
  tell us the real wait.
- **Recognition / AI quality** — OCR accuracy on real signs, object/scene ID, summarisation
  usefulness, and **emotion-AI accuracy** (known shaky — `05` §3) are **model-gated**; only real
  models on real frames answer "is the result good enough to be trustworthy?"
- **Low-light edge outlining** — whether bright additive outlines actually help in a real dim
  scene without occluding the hazard (G4) needs the real optic in real low light; daylight
  washout is a physics fact but the *useful* threshold is empirical.
- **Field-loss arrow legibility** at the residual-field edge, and binocular-rivalry on the
  monocular optic — real wearer, real field loss.
- **Felt cognitive load** — whether each overlay *actually* reduces effort for the cohort is the
  collaborator's lived-experience test on hardware (`collaborator-and-codesign.md`).
- **Real gaze accuracy / calibration drift** for the low-vision cohort specifically (§6.2).
- The simulator is the **product surface** until hardware ships and must never lie about these;
  all above are deferred to hardware + the collaborator-tester, **not** claimed done from the sim.

### 6.6 Eye-tracking UI patterns (Eyefluence-informed) + FTO / patent-posture check

*Hook for Peter Milford's Eyefluence expertise (Google-acquired Eyefluence; gaze-interaction
models), to be folded in as Peter-authored notes (goals §8 open-Q 4, spec §19).* For this
layer the most relevant Eyefluence-informed asks: **how to make "look-then-ask" feel
intentional, not Midas** (the wearer's eyes are on the world the whole time); the lowest-
fatigue summon gesture for a recognition request; and confirmation models for capture that
don't require a precise second fixation.

**FTO / patent posture.** Concordance Lens introduces **no new gaze-activation mechanism** — it
reuses the PMEyeUI FTO bundle's widget-level implementation (**A1 + B8 + C2 + E1–E4**: the B8
morph and C2 follow target) through the **shared** widget layer, exactly as the substrate
mandates. Per-feature drawers here declare **nouns + visual assets only**; they do **not** fork
the pursuit/reveal animation (patent-posture regression — `feedback_widget_level_patent_guard`).
The COACH inferred-prompt and the recognition card are **content** rendered by shared widgets,
not new activation grammar. Enforced by `tools/fto-check` in the precommit-walk.

## 7. Red lines & community position (R1–R6)

- **R3 — No surveillance of vulnerable users; memory aids reduce dependence, never create it.**
  This layer is squarely in the population the GPS-tracking-of-dementia literature warns about
  (`05` §1, §6 goblin 5). So: **no caregiver-monitoring dashboard, no silent exfiltration, no
  "compliance tracking."** Name/face recall (F6) is **wearer-summoned, private, never logged,
  never broadcast.** Wayfinding (F6) cues **durable landmarks to teach spatial skill** rather
  than fostering turn-by-turn dependence (NavMarkAR — `05` §5). Caregiver *configuration* of the
  routine prompter (F3) is support-side help (so a dementia user need not learn an interface),
  **never** a feed of what the wearer did. The wearer is always sender and owner.
- **R4 — Contested features off-by-default, wearer-owned.** Emotion/affect (F11) ships **OFF**,
  wearer-only, accuracy-honest, never a coach, never a caregiver feed (§5). Designed *with* the
  community ("nothing about us without us" — ASAN), against the autistic-autonomy critique.
- **Dementia learnability + dignity.** Extreme simplicity, consistency, no hidden gestures,
  audio support, support-side configuration (§6.4). Memory aids carry stigma + a dependence
  risk; glasses are *less* visibly stigmatising than a hovering caregiver **if discreet** — a
  dignity win the design must protect (`05` §5).
- **Privacy (G6, spec §15).** **No always-on camera; clear capture indicator whenever the
  camera is active; no cloud vision by default; no image storage by default.** Frames are
  analysed and discarded unless the wearer explicitly saves; cloud is explicit/minimal/visible/
  revocable; settings stay local; the wearer can delete everything. **R5:** no recording of
  bystanders without lawful consent; two-party-consent jurisdictions respected.
- **R1 — Not a medical device.** No diagnosis, no vision/hearing-restoration claim; copy audited
  like `claims-audit` (G7). **R2 — Never a mobility-aid replacement** (F9/F10): the HUD is a
  low-light *supplement*; haptic/audio is the primary hazard channel; **keep using your cane /
  guide dog** is first-run + persistent messaging; in bright light overlays are limited (`03`
  §6, design-impl 7). **R6 — Accessibility is never a hidden mode**: Concordance is part of the
  one product, not an "accessibility build."

**Community position (G8):** co-design is an **active gate**. The collaborator (lived-experience
domain partner + tester) reviews the open questions (§10) — especially the emotion/affect and
memory-aid features — **before** they lock; lived-experience feedback outranks researcher
assumptions. Because this is the highest-risk layer, **no contested feature here lands without
explicit community co-design sign-off**, not just a researcher citation.

## 8. Multiplatform & multidevice (#330)

Concordance Lens is built on the shared core and inherits the #330 cross-platform contract
(`docs/pme-eye-ui/plan-kmp-cross-platform.md`; substrate §8):

- **Angular units (degrees) + milliseconds only — NO pixel/frame constants in logic.** Card
  sizes, the ambient-time arc, the look-here arrow, the edge-outline stroke, the dwell
  affordance are in **degrees**; capture/recognition timeouts, working-state animation, card
  auto-clear, dwell ~600 ms are in **ms**. A `const val FOO_PX` / `BAR_FRAMES` in logic is a
  portability defect; pixel/scale conversion happens only at the sink boundary (`AngleMapper`).
- **Dual-sink parity — phone preview ≡ HMD render, line-for-line.** The same `HomeSnapshot`
  (carrying Concordance state: pending capture, working/queued state, the result card, the
  COACH step, the ambient-time arc value, the look-here arrow bearing) renders identically on
  the M2 HMD sink and `PhoneCanvasSink`. No `drawXPreview` divergence.
- **Android + iOS + Raven** via the shared core (#330 Phases 4/6): the Concordance reducer +
  widgets are platform-free (`commonMain`); only sinks and device adapters are `actual`.
- **Device matrix:** **Maverick v1 (640×400, ~22°)**, **v2 (1280×720, ~28°)**, **Raven (~30°
  off-axis)** — every metric angular, so the layer paints to all three with **no widget edits**;
  only sink-level scale changes. The look-here arrow + edge outline respect each device's FOV.
- **Library-first widgets (G10).** The result card, COACH step card, recall card, word-finding
  scaffold, ambient-time arc, look-here arrow, and edge-outline overlay are **shared widgets**
  reachable by every renderer + the synthesized-app generator (`shared-pattern-audit`) — never
  private to `HmdRenderer`. Where any of these is genuinely cross-app (e.g. the result card),
  it lands in the shared library, not in a Concordance-private drawer.
- **`scene_snapshot` parity oracle.** `tools/parity-snapshot` drives the same controller state
  (a pending capture, a working state, a result card, a COACH step, the ambient arc, the
  look-here arrow) through each platform's sink and diffs the sim's platform-neutral
  `scene_snapshot` M2 tree vs golden + cross-platform; the build fails on divergence. Scene-graph
  text drawables are the device-faithful oracle for "is the card/arc/arrow actually present and
  correct."
- **The recognition / AI backend is a per-platform / cloud-optional ADAPTER behind a shared
  interface.** Concordance declares a `RecognitionRequest → RecognitionResult` boundary in
  `commonMain` (text-read / object-id / scene-summary / focus-query / affect, each with a
  confidence + uncertainty field). Concrete adapters are `actual` per platform (on-device ML on
  Android/iOS; a different stack on Raven) and may be **cloud-optional** — but **on-device-first /
  no cloud by default** (G6, spec §5.5). The shared layer never assumes cloud; the sim uses a
  stub adapter; swapping adapters never touches the widgets, the reducer, or the parity tests.

## 9. Test plan (5 dimensions; ASCII `[x]`/`[ ]`, per sub-state)

Per `CLAUDE.md` (5 dimensions, per **sub-state**, driven through the **real** dispatch path —
`step:*` seams + `adb input` / `InputSignal` injection — with **both** sinks captured + read
inline and the active-area fraction stated in prose). Everything is `[ ]` (ASPIRATIONAL — no
code; this is a LATER layer). **Sub-states covered:** idle/quiet · COACH stall-triggered prompt
· wearer-summoned card · capture+analysis · name/face recall · ambient-time arc · low-light
edge-outline · field-loss arrow · emotion-recog ON · emotion-recog OFF · empty/no-result ·
recognition-fail · camera-permission-denied.

### 9.1 Functionality (real dispatch path, per sub-state)
- [ ] `IdleQuietContributesNothingTest` — at rest Concordance adds **zero** drawables (only the
      substrate Status Bead persists); ambient-time arc shows **only** when opted-in.
- [ ] `CoachStallPromptInferredOnlyTest` — the COACH step (F3) appears **only** on an inferred
      stall (dwell/hesitation/GPS-time), one step at a time, then clears; **commits nothing** (L0).
- [ ] `SummonedCardPullOnlyTest` — F4 cards appear **only** on an explicit pull (gaze-summon /
      gesture / voice); never auto-push; fade in (never jump); single-glance; auto-clear.
- [ ] `CaptureAnalysisFlowTest` — summon → explicit capture (indicator shown) → working state →
      result card (F1/F2); routed at the declared **L1/L2** through the substrate Safety Gate.
- [ ] `NameFaceRecallOnRequestTest` — F6 recall card appears **only on the wearer's request**
      when looking at a pre-enrolled person; never logged/broadcast (R3).
- [ ] `AmbientTimeArcTest` — F7 arc renders at the fixed peripheral position, shrinks over the
      configured window, single-glance; opt-in; counts against overlay budget.
- [ ] `WordFindingScaffoldTest` — F8 surfaces a small relevant noun/icon set on request; never
      auto-speaks for the user.
- [ ] `LowLightEdgeOutlineGatesOnLuxTest` — F9 outlines a hazard **edge** (never fills/occludes,
      G4) **only** below the dark-scene lux threshold; **auto-disables** in bright light.
- [ ] `FieldLossArrowTest` — F10 draws a single high-luminance edge arrow toward an off-axis
      target; attention-cue only, does not redraw the scene; respects FOV.
- [ ] `EmotionRecogDefaultOffTest` — F11 is **OFF** on first run; cannot fire until explicitly
      enabled; when on, on-request snapshot only, output to wearer only.
- [ ] `RedundantInputEquivalenceTest` — F1/F4/F11 complete via **every** modality {gaze+confirm,
      head-nod, voice, tap/switch, phone}; same result regardless (G1).
- [ ] `SelectHighlightOnEveryTargetTest` — every selectable result/target shows a select
      highlight (eye-tracking law); full-screen affordances (reveal/Back/Home/BottomMenu) present.

### 9.2 Appearance (BOTH sinks — HMD fullpage + AVD screencap, read inline; state active-area fraction)
- [ ] `ResultCardParityTest` — F1/F2 card renders phone↔HMD parity; text legible; colours clear
      the additive floor; HMD fill ≥20% where a backdrop is painted; capture indicator visible.
- [ ] `CoachStepParityTest` / `RecallCardParityTest` / `WordScaffoldParityTest` — each card
      identical angular position/size on both sinks; no overlap with the Status Bead or captions.
- [ ] `AmbientArcParityTest` — the arc animates identically on both sinks at the fixed position.
- [ ] `EdgeOutlineParityTest` — the bright edge outline (F9) and look-here arrow (F10) render at
      the same angular position on both sinks; outline never fills the hazard region (G4).
- [ ] `DwellAffordanceCountdownParityTest` — the counting-down dwell affordance animates on both
      sinks (reversible actions only).
- [ ] `UiOverlapBoundsSweepTest` extension — every Concordance overlay (cards, arc, arrow,
      outline, working state) never overlaps content / exits active bounds, across C/D configs.

### 9.3 Error / empty / loading (FORCE each)
- [ ] `EmptyNoResultTest` — recognition returns nothing → an honest "couldn't find anything"
      card, no overflow, no phantom result, clears cleanly.
- [ ] `RecognitionFailTest` — the recognition adapter errors/times out → clean state +
      "couldn't read that" status; **no half-result**; the working state never hangs (atomicity).
- [ ] `CameraPermissionDeniedTest` — capture with camera permission denied → a clear "camera
      unavailable" state + how to grant; **no crash, no capture indicator stuck on** (forces the
      `03`/`05` error path; the long unusual string must fit — #310 overflow class).
- [ ] `CloudDisabledOnDeviceFallbackTest` — with `cloud_services_enabled=false`, F2 stays
      on-device-first; no silent cloud call; if a feature *needs* cloud it says so and stays off.
- [ ] `LowConfidenceUncertaintyShownTest` — a low-confidence F1/F11 result **shows uncertainty /
      never asserts** (the accuracy-honest rule; emotion-AI shakiness — `05` §3).
- [ ] `WorkingStateNeverBlocksTest` — a slow recognition shows a working/queued state, stays
      cancellable, and never blocks the UI or busts the frame budget.

### 9.4 Timing
- [ ] `CardAutoClearTimingTest` — summoned cards auto-clear on their ms spec; timeout dismisses,
      never commits.
- [ ] `AtRestAllocationTest` extension — Concordance adds **zero** per-frame rebuild/alloc at
      rest (#302/#297); the ambient arc, when on, is cheap.
- [ ] `FrameBudgetTest` — card open / arc tick / outline draw / arrow draw stay within the #222
      frame budget; no cold-rebuild freeze on capture.
- [ ] `CaptureLatencyHarnessTest` — measures the on-request capture→result latency against the
      declared budget (real value is hardware/model-gated — §6.5 / 9.7).

### 9.5 Failed use cases
- [ ] `NoInferredCaptureTest` — gaze dwell on a face/sign **never** fires a capture or query
      (gaze-is-attention); only the explicit summon does.
- [ ] `MistakenCaptureDiscardedTest` — a mistaken capture is discarded (no storage by default,
      R5) / undoable; nothing persists.
- [ ] `EmotionRecogNeverCoachNeverFeedTest` — F11 output goes **only** to the wearer; never a
      coach instruction; never written to a caregiver feed or a remote log (R4/R3).
- [ ] `NoSurprisePushTest` — no Concordance surface (except the inferred, wearer-pre-approved,
      mutable F3) ever appears unbidden in the central FOV (`05` §5 sensory-aggressor rule).
- [ ] `OverlayBudgetEnforcedTest` — over-summoning cannot exceed the substrate overlay budget;
      quiet-mode mutes all but the essential.
- [ ] `NoLeakBetweenSurfacesTest` — a pending capture / result card / arc does not leak across
      X→Home→Y (`scene_snapshot` clean; #199 app→app leak class).
- [ ] `DoubleSummonIdempotentTest` — rapid double-summon produces at most one capture/card.
- [ ] **Cognitive-load proxies (§6.4):** `ElementsOnScreenBudgetTest`, `TimeToClearTest`
      (single-glance), `ActsPerCaptureTest` — a cognitive layer's value is not a screenshot.

### 9.6 Cross-platform & per-device-class parity (#330)
- [ ] `ConcordanceScenePARITY_AndroidVsIos` — same input snapshot (pending capture, result card,
      COACH step, ambient arc, look-here arrow) → same `scene_snapshot` on Android vs iOS.
- [ ] `ConcordanceScenePARITY_AndroidVsRaven` — same, Android vs Raven (pixel/SSIM if no
      scene_snapshot-equivalent).
- [ ] `ConcordanceDeviceClassParity` — widgets render at correct angular size on **v1
      (640×400/22°)**, **v2 (1280×720/28°)**, **Raven (~30°)** with no widget edits.
- [ ] `RecognitionAdapterBoundaryTest` — the `RecognitionRequest→RecognitionResult` interface is
      platform-free in `commonTest`; swapping the stub adapter never touches widgets/reducer/parity.

### 9.7 Hardware / model-only (cannot be honestly validated on the simulator — §6.5)
- [ ] **(hardware)** real on-request capture latency + felt "look→ask→wait" experience.
- [ ] **(model)** recognition quality — OCR / object-id / summary usefulness; **emotion-AI
      accuracy** (known shaky — `05` §3); is the result trustworthy enough to surface?
- [ ] **(hardware/optic)** low-light edge-outline usefulness without hazard occlusion (G4);
      field-loss arrow legibility at the residual-field edge; monocular binocular-rivalry.
- [ ] **(hardware + collaborator)** felt **cognitive** load — does each overlay reduce or add
      effort for the cohort? (the most important validation this layer needs).
- [ ] **(hardware)** real gaze accuracy / calibration drift for the low-vision cohort (§6.2).

## 10. Open design questions

In-doc; the collaborator reviews these before any contested feature locks (§7, G8).

1. **Emotion/affect (F11) — build it at all?** Even fully governed (§5), it is the most
   contested feature in the suite and the autistic community broadly rejects the framing. **The
   most important open question:** does co-design with the served communities actually want it,
   or is the right answer to ship only the predictability/control features (§3) and **not** the
   emotion read? Lived-experience sign-off, not a researcher citation, decides (G8).
2. **Recognition/AI backend — on-device feasibility vs cloud.** Which features (text-read,
   object-id, scene-summary, focus-query, affect) are good enough **on-device** on the v1/v2/
   Raven hardware, and which would *need* cloud (and so default off / require explicit consent)?
   Determines how much of §4 is realistic without breaking "no cloud by default."
3. **COACH stall-inference signal.** What actually constitutes a reliable "stall" (dwell time?
   GPS+time? task model?) without false-positive nagging — the line between a helpful just-in-
   time cue and a reminder-fatigue / sensory-aggressor pop-up (`05` §5,6). Needs the cohort.
4. **Name/face recall enrolment + consent (F6).** How are faces pre-enrolled, where is that data
   stored (wearer-owned, local — R3), and what is the *bystander* consent posture when the camera
   captures a face for recall? Sharpest privacy question after F11.
5. **Capture-indicator sufficiency (R5).** Is an in-lens indicator enough for bystander consent,
   or does the platform/jurisdiction require an external (outward-facing) indicator? Affects
   whether F1/F2/F6/F11 are lawful in two-party-consent jurisdictions.
6. **Ambient-time arc as the one always-present element** — does its persistence survive the
   overlay-budget discipline in practice, or does even one always-on mark violate "calm by
   absence" for some users? Make it strictly opt-in and per-user (current design), pending cohort.

### Top 3 for the user (Peter)
- **Q1 — ship emotion/affect at all?** It is the highest-risk feature in the suite; the
  community position may be "don't," and that outranks the researchers. Needs an explicit
  co-design decision before any code.
- **Q2 — on-device vs cloud recognition feasibility.** Decides how much of the §14 feature set
  is buildable without breaking "no cloud / no storage by default" (G6); shapes the adapter.
- **Q5 — capture-indicator / bystander-consent sufficiency.** An in-lens-only indicator may be
  legally insufficient in two-party-consent jurisdictions; gates whether the recognition
  features are lawful at all.
