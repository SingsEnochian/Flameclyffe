**Status:** ASPIRATIONAL — design only, no code. No Sentinel Lantern surface exists in the
repo. This PRD is the design contract; nothing here is "implemented" until earned by code +
the proving tests below (`tools/claims-audit` rules apply). Many numbers (latency, dwell,
countdown durations) are **assumptions-to-verify** against the final Maverick SDK + hardware —
each is tagged `[VERIFY]`.

Source of intent: L. Catherine Dion's Lantern Stack spec v0.1 §12
(`source-spec-lantern-stack-v0.1.md`), reconciled with the Lantern design law
(`goals-and-guidelines.md`). Sentinel is **§7.C** of the slate and the **highest-risk** Lantern
app — it is where the Safety Gate earns its keep. **Two design decisions are on record here

feedback**, not a sim stub (§8); (2) the aid flow is reframed as a non-medical **"Reliable Contact"**

Research grounding: `research/03-lowvision-navigation-safety.md` (§4 — pre-syncope/SOS, now a

## 0. Substrate ownership (read first — do not redefine here)

The **Lantern substrate** (`app-prd-lantern-substrate.md`, parallel, OWNER) defines and owns,
once, in the shared library:

- the **Safety Gate** + the **Risk Levels L0–L5** taxonomy (spec §8);
- **redundant input** (G1 — every action reachable by ≥2 of {gaze-select, head gesture, voice,
  tap/switch, phone}; never mandatory fusion);
- the **confirm / cancel / undo** patterns + the **protected confirmation card** (spec §9);
- **panic-cancel** (hardware button / emergency-stop gesture);
- **Low-Energy / reclined-bed / minimal-head-movement** posture modes (G3, spec §16);
- the **Status Bead** default-HUD element (spec §10);
- the `ActionIntent` / `SafetyEvent` / `UserSettings` data model (spec §7).

Sentinel **references** these. It **declares each action's Risk Level**, **assembles** the
substrate's shared widgets, and **does not** invent its own activation/confirm/cancel machinery.
A per-app confirm card, a per-app dwell ring, a per-app countdown timer = a substrate violation
(`tools/shared-pattern-audit`-class) and a patent-posture regression (widget-level invariants,
not per-app). Sentinel's job is **nouns + intent + Risk-Level tags + recipient/device config**.

## 1. Purpose

Sentinel Lantern lets a **hands-reduced / bedbound / fatigue / chronically-ill** wearer do two
things, hands-free and voice-optional, when hands or voice aren't reliable:

1. **Control their immediate environment** — lights on/off, fan on/off, simple IoT — without
   a phone, without standing up, without speaking. (`L3`.)
2. **Reach a pre-chosen reliable contact** — a wearer-INITIATED message or call to a trusted
   person the wearer configured in advance ("Reliable Contact"); optionally Alexa Drop In (if the
   wearer set it up). This is framed as a **communication convenience for hands-reduced use** —
   *"reach your person"* — **NOT** an emergency / medical / SOS / "summon help" function. The wearer
   is **always the sender and owner**. (`L4`.)

The point is **agency in a moment of low capacity**: the bedbound user turns the light off
without reaching; the fatigued user reaches their partner without finding the phone or forming
words. **Reliable Contact is convenience, not a lifeline** — it does not promise that help
arrives, and nothing in Sentinel is framed as emergency or medical (R1).

## 2. Target users, scope, non-goals

**Target users** (spec §4): limited hand mobility; post-surgery/injury recovery; chronically ill
(fatigue/pain/fluctuating capacity, ME/CFS, MS, EDS, POTS); bedbound/reclined/low-movement; users
who sometimes can't speak clearly. **Secondary:** the household/Reliable Contact who *receives* a
wearer-sent message (never watches the wearer).

**In scope (MVP, spec §12):** lights on/off + fan on/off (only if connected AND safe);
a preconfigured **Reliable Contact** message/call to a pre-chosen trusted person (a
hands-reduced communication convenience, **not** SOS); Alexa Drop In or equivalent (only if
user-configured); a **visible safety lock**; a **testing mode**.

**Later (spec §12):** caregiver dashboard (CAUTION — R3, see §5), routine check-ins, escalation
ladder, fall-risk/immobility context (opt-in only), broader smart-home ecosystem adapters (§8).

**Deferred / out of scope (would require medical validation; never claim medical/emergency
reliability — R1):** the `03` low-vision **pre-syncope brace** prompt and any **L5 emergency /
countdown SOS** (with GPS / Medical ID). These are **removed from MVP.** They remain documented as
a *future-with-validation* item only: building them at all requires co-design **plus** a real
medical-validation plan, and even then Sentinel must never present them as a guaranteed lifeline.
Reliable Contact deliberately does **not** cover this ground — it is "reach your person," not
"summon emergency help." See §3.4, §11.5.

**Non-goals (red lines — violating these is a defect, not a tradeoff):**

- **NOT a medical or emergency-response device (R1).** Sentinel does not detect falls reliably,
  does not monitor vitals as a medical function, and **must not claim emergency/medical
  reliability** in any surface or doc. **Reliable Contact is a communication convenience, never an
  SOS/lifeline** — it is a wearer-initiated message/call to a pre-chosen person, framed as "reach
  your person," and it is **not** marketed or worded as "summon help" / "call for help" /
  emergency. (Emergency/SOS and pre-syncope brace are removed from MVP, §2.) Marketing/onboarding
  say so. Language audited like `claims-audit` (G7).
- **NOT surveillance of the wearer (R3).** No caregiver-facing monitoring dashboard, no silent
  data exfiltration, no compliance tracking, no "is grandma up yet" feed. See §5.
- **NO Reliable-Contact flow from gaze alone** (spec §3, R3). **NO appliance activation
  without explicit confirm** (spec §5.1). **Coffee-maker-class devices (anything that heats,
  starts a motor, or is unsafe to leave on) are DEFERRED** until reliable device-state feedback
  exists (spec §12) — MVP devices are limited to lights + fan, which are safe to toggle blind.
  (Note §8: Matter subscriptions / Hue SSE / HA WebSocket now provide that state feedback, so the
  deferral can be lifted **per-device, when proven on a real adapter**, not by fiat.)
- **NOT a home-automation platform.** Sentinel is a *safe control surface* over a small set of
  the wearer's *already-configured* devices/contacts. It does not discover, provision, or manage
  a smart home.

## 3. UX — room control, Reliable Contact, the safety lock, testing mode, status

### 3.1 Default HUD (quiet by default — G2)

At rest Sentinel draws **nothing** beyond the substrate **Status Bead** (which may carry the
**safety-lock** glyph and any **caregiver-flow state**). No persistent menu. Quiet-by-default is
mandatory; the additive optic cannot render a "dim" recessive panel, so calm = *absent until
summoned* (G2). The Sentinel control strip is **summoned** (gaze-to-bead / head / voice / tap),
not always shown.

### 3.2 Room control (L3) — lights / fan / simple IoT

A summoned **device strip**: one large card per configured device (≥ config floor in *both* axes,
spec §10 "no tiny buttons"), each showing **device name + current state if known** (e.g. "Bedroom
lamp — ON"). Flow:

1. **Focus** (gaze / head / pointer) highlights a device card — `L0`, no consequence, just a
   selection highlight (every selectable card shows the substrate selection highlight).
2. **ACT** stages the intended change (toggle) and opens the substrate **confirmation card**:
   *named target* + *action* + a visible **countdown** (`[VERIFY]` ~3 s) + explicit **confirm**
   signal + **cancel-until-execution**. This is **two-step, no gaze-only** (G1/L3) — the confirm
   must come from a *non-gaze-includable* second signal (head-nod / voice "yes" / tap / switch);
   a gaze saccade alone may select but **may not** be the L3 confirm.
3. **CONFIRM** executes; the card shows **"Bedroom lamp → ON"** result + an **undo** affordance
   (lights/fan are reversible). If no device-state feedback is available, the result line says
   **"sent — state unknown"**, never a false "ON".

**Device safety rules (L3):** only devices the wearer pre-registered as `room_devices` appear;
only **safe-to-toggle-blind** classes (light, fan) are enabled in MVP. Coffee-maker-class
appears **greyed with a "needs device feedback" note**, never actionable (claim honesty).

### 3.3 Reliable Contact (L4) — reach a pre-chosen person (NOT SOS)

A summoned **Reliable-Contact strip** (visually distinct from the room strip — different color
band, a contact glyph), one card per configured contact/flow. This is a **wearer-initiated
message or call to a pre-chosen trusted person** — a *communication convenience for hands-reduced
use*, **not** emergency/medical/SOS. Wording throughout is "reach your person," never "summon
help." Flow (substrate protected confirmation card, `L4`):

1. **Focus** highlights the contact card — `L0`.
2. **ACT** opens the **protected confirmation card**: **visible recipient** ("Reach: Jess
   (sister)"), the action ("Send message" / "Call"), a **cancel countdown** (`[VERIFY]` ~5 s,
   longer than L3), explicit **confirm** signal, **cancel-until-execution**. **No silent trigger;
   no gaze-only** (G1/L4) — confirm requires an explicit non-gaze second signal.
3. **CONFIRM** sends/calls, then shows **post-action status**: **"Sent to Jess — confirmed"** /
   **"…— no confirmation yet"** / **"Failed — retry?"** (the honest four-status model, §3.7).
   Reliable Contact shows **visible recipient confirmation** of receipt where the channel returns
   a receipt; where it doesn't, the status says so honestly (no fake "confirmed"). It never claims
   the contact will respond or that help is coming — it reports only what the *channel* reports.

Drop In (if `cloud_services_enabled` + a configured target): same `L4` card; the recipient is the
Drop-In endpoint; status shows the connection state. Still framed as "reach your person," not SOS.

### 3.4 Deferred — pre-syncope brace + L5 emergency SOS (REMOVED from MVP)

**Removed from MVP (user direction, 2026-06-09).** The `03` §4 **pre-syncope "find a seat / brace"
prompt** and any **L5 countdown SOS** (GPS + Medical ID escalation) are **not built** and are not
part of the Reliable-Contact feature. They are documented here only as a **future-with-validation**
possibility: building them at all would require co-design **plus** a real medical-validation plan,
and — load-bearing — they **MUST NOT claim medical/emergency reliability without validation (R1)**.
The low-vision pre-syncope idea from `research/03` stays a documented future item, never an MVP
deliverable. Reliable Contact (§3.3) intentionally does **not** approximate an SOS: it is "reach
your person," not "summon emergency help," precisely so Sentinel makes no emergency/medical claim.

### 3.5 The visible safety lock

A **wearer-controlled lock** (spec §12) shown in the Status Bead. While **locked**, all `L3+`
actions are unavailable (focus still previews, but ACT is refused with "Sentinel is locked").
Unlock is itself a deliberate non-gaze action. Purpose: a fatigued/sleeping/seizing wearer, or
one whose gaze is wandering, cannot fire a real action; the lock is the coarse Midas-touch
backstop *on top of* the per-action ceremony. Default state and auto-relock timeout are
co-design questions (§11).

### 3.6 Testing mode (Catherine §18 — exercise the L4 Reliable-Contact flow WITHOUT sending)

A first-class, **clearly-indicated** mode (distinct banner color + a persistent "TEST MODE" tag
in the Bead) in which **every Reliable-Contact flow runs the full UI — recipient card, countdown,
confirm, cancel, post-action status — but the External Service Adapter is a no-op stub**: nothing
is sent. The result line reads **"TEST — nothing was sent"**. This lets the wearer (and Catherine
as tester) rehearse the exact flow, learn the timings, and verify cancel/panic-cancel, with zero
risk of a real message/call going out. Room control (`L3`) in test mode actuates the **sim-stub
device** (§8 — demo/offline only), never a real bulb. Testing mode is **per-session, never sticky
into real use silently** — leaving it requires an explicit toggle and the Bead tag clears.

### 3.7 "What just happened" — nothing-sent vs sent (load-bearing, spec §5.4, §18)

Every Sentinel action ends in an **unambiguous, persistent-until-dismissed status line**, because
the failure mode here is catastrophic (a wearer who thinks help is coming when it isn't, or who
fired an alert they meant to cancel). The four canonical statuses, each visually distinct:

| Status | When | Copy |
|---|---|---|
| **NOTHING SENT** | cancelled / timed out / panic-cancel / test mode | "Cancelled — nothing was sent." / "TEST — nothing was sent." |
| **SENT** | dispatched, awaiting receipt | "Sent to Jess — waiting for confirmation…" |
| **CONFIRMED** | recipient/channel confirmed receipt | "Jess got it." |
| **FAILED** | dispatch error | "Couldn't send — retry?" |

"Nothing sent" and "Sent" must never be visually confusable. Atomicity (§4) guarantees a
cancelled/timed-out action leaves **no** SafetyEvent-as-sent and **no** partial dispatch.

## 4. Risk-Level mapping for EVERY action

Each action declares its Risk Level; the Safety Gate enforces the row. `[VERIFY]` = timing to
confirm on hardware.

| Action | Risk | Gate behaviour (enforced by substrate) |
|---|---|---|
| Summon device/Reliable-Contact strip; focus a card; preview state | **L0** | no confirm; easy dismiss; selection highlight only |
| Toggle safety lock (lock direction) | **L1** | simple confirm; undo (unlock) |
| Toggle safety lock (unlock direction) | **L2** | confirm (deliberate, non-gaze); it re-arms actions, so treat as persistent |
| Enter/leave testing mode | **L2** | confirm; persistent; clear Bead indicator |
| **Light on/off** | **L3** | two-step; named target; countdown `[VERIFY]`~3 s; **no gaze-only**; cancel-until-exec; undo; device safety rules |
| **Fan on/off** | **L3** | as above |
| Coffee-maker-class | **deferred** | greyed; not actionable until reliable device-state feedback (R1 honesty) |
| **Reliable Contact — message / call a pre-chosen person** | **L4** | strong confirm; **visible recipient**; cancel countdown `[VERIFY]`~5 s; **no silent trigger; no gaze-only**; post-action status; visible recipient confirmation. **NOT SOS** — framed "reach your person" (R1) |
| **Alexa Drop In (if configured)** | **L4** | as L4; recipient = Drop-In target; connection-state status; still "reach your person," not SOS |
| **Pre-syncope brace prompt / L5 emergency SOS** | **deferred (REMOVED from MVP)** | future-with-validation only; never built without co-design + a medical-validation plan; **never claims medical/emergency reliability (R1)** (§3.4) |

**Protected confirmation card (L3+):** always shows **recipient/device + action + countdown +
explicit confirm + cancel-until-execution**. **Panic-cancel** (hardware button / emergency-stop
gesture, substrate-owned) aborts any pending or counting-down action at any instant and lands on
the **NOTHING SENT** status. Panic-cancel is reachable **without gaze** by definition.

## 5. Caregiver vs surveillance (R3) — handled explicitly

**The line (R3, verbatim intent):** *the wearer is always the sender and owner, never the
surveilled. A caregiver may RECEIVE a wearer-sent alert but never WATCH the wearer.*

Design consequences, non-negotiable:

- **No monitoring dashboard ships.** There is no caregiver-facing app that shows the wearer's
  state, location, activity, "last seen", or compliance. The only thing a Reliable Contact ever
  gets is a **discrete message the wearer explicitly sent** (an SMS/call/notification/Drop-In *the
  wearer confirmed*), plus whatever that one message contains.
- **Every outbound flow is wearer-initiated and wearer-confirmed.** There is no "auto-notify if
  no movement for N hours" in MVP. Even the later opt-in fall-risk/immobility context (§10) must
  be **wearer-owned, default-off, and only ever surfaces a prompt *to the wearer* who then
  chooses to send** — it never sends on the wearer's behalf silently.
- **The wearer owns and can revoke recipients.** `safe_contacts` are wearer-configured, locally
  stored, wearer-deletable; removing a contact removes their ability to be sent to. No recipient
  can add themselves or request data.
- **Pull, never push, of state.** Nothing about the wearer's state leaves the device except
  inside a message the wearer confirmed. No background telemetry, no location beacon, no
  "presence" channel (G6).
- **If a caregiver dashboard is ever built (later, CAUTION):** it must be **wearer-owned and
  wearer-explicit** — the wearer chooses to share specific, named, time-bounded things, sees
  exactly what's shared, and can revoke instantly. A dashboard that the *caregiver* controls or
  that shows anything the wearer didn't choose to share is an R3 violation and does not ship.
  This bar is high enough that MVP simply does not include it.

The community grounding (`05` §5–6, G8): vulnerable-user distrust of "monitoring" is universal;
memory/safety aids must **reduce dependence, not create it**. Sentinel's safety value comes from
*the wearer's amplified agency*, not from someone watching them.

## 6. Collaborator's required sections

### 6.1 Accidental-activation avoidance (CRITICAL here — no accidental Reliable-Contact sends / toggles)

This is the app where Midas-touch is most dangerous. Layers, all substrate-provided, stacked:

1. **Gaze is attention, not consent (spec §5.1).** Gaze only focuses/previews. It can **never**
   be the confirm for `L3+`. The substrate enforces "no gaze-only" on these levels.
2. **Visible safety lock (§3.5)** — a coarse global off-switch the wearer holds; while locked,
   no real action can fire regardless of gaze.
3. **Two-step + named target + countdown (L3) / protected card + recipient + longer countdown
   (L4).** Ceremony scales to consequence (spec §8). (No L5 in MVP — §3.4.)
4. **Non-gaze confirm requirement** for `L3+` — the second signal must be a deliberate
   head-nod / voice / tap / switch, decoupled from "looking" (the research-favored Midas breaker,
   `04` §2). A wandering or fatigued gaze cannot complete the action.
5. **Cancel-until-execution + panic-cancel + timeout-dismisses** — an action mid-countdown is
   abortable by any input incl. look-away/timeout, and the hardware panic-cancel always wins.
6. **Debounce / ignore-repeat** (substrate, `04` §4) — a single intent never double-fires; a
   tremor/jitter can't re-trigger a just-cancelled flow.
7. **Testing mode** so rehearsal never risks a real send.

Failure to defeat accidental activation here is not a polish gap — it is a safety defect. The
test plan's accidental-activation **fuzz** (§10) is mandatory and HARD.

### 6.2 Calibration assumptions

- **Assumed gaze accuracy:** the project's config floor (≥2.8°/3.2° in both axes); Sentinel cards
  are sized at or above it. **Assumption-to-verify** against the final SDK's reported gaze
  accuracy (`[VERIFY]`).
- **This population breaks standard calibration** (`03` §6, `04` §6): nystagmus, strabismus,
  eccentric PRL, ME/CFS jerky pursuit, dry eye/ptosis. So Sentinel **does not assume gaze
  calibrates** for its users. Every action is reachable by the G1 non-gaze paths (head / voice /
  switch / phone) hitting the *same* Safety Gate. A wearer who can't calibrate gaze can still lock
  the house, toggle a light, and reach their Reliable Contact — **and the L3+ "no gaze-only" rule
  means this is not even a degraded path, it's a first-class one.**
- **Graceful degradation (spec §6):** on low ET confidence / drift / dropout, Sentinel falls back
  to non-gaze selection automatically and shows the substrate "gaze unavailable" affordance;
  pending actions are never auto-confirmed by a recovering/noisy gaze signal.

### 6.3 Dwell alternatives (NO gaze-only for L3+)

- **No `L3+` action uses dwell as its confirm.** Dwell is gaze-only by nature; the "no gaze-only"
  rule forbids it for room control, aid, and emergency. Confirm = head-nod / voice / tap / switch
  (substrate), or the project saccade-to-follow-target as the *select* step (not the L3+ commit).
- **Where dwell appears at all** (e.g. dismissing an `L0` preview), it is only on **fully
  reversible** targets and shows the **substrate counting-down affordance**; duration is tunable
  (`[VERIFY]` ~0.6 s) and a non-dwell path exists in parallel (G1, `04` goblin #2/#7).
- **Calibration-light smooth-pursuit** (Pursuits/Orbits, `04` §2) is the preferred *selection*
  primitive for the strip where it lowers effort — but it still routes into a non-gaze **confirm**
  for `L3+`. Pursuit selects; it never commits a consequential action alone.

### 6.4 Fatigue / energy budget (G3)

- **Resting cost ~zero:** at rest Sentinel is the Bead only; no per-frame work, no polling of
  devices that isn't event-driven (the substrate's at-rest-allocation discipline applies).
- **Low-Energy mode (substrate):** larger targets, longer/forgiving countdowns and timeouts,
  head-nod/voice confirm preferred over sustained gaze, fewer cards shown at once. Toggleable from
  the phone so a wearer on a bad day sets it without HUD interaction (`04` impl #1).
- **Reclined/bed + minimal-head-movement modes (G3, spec §16):** the device strip and
  Reliable-Contact strip must be reachable and confirmable **without head turns** and **while
  reclined** — this is the bedbound user's core scenario. The substrate owns these modes; Sentinel
  must not place a card or a confirm target that requires a head turn to reach in
  minimal-head-movement mode.
- **Resumability:** a flow interrupted by PEM/brain-fog/look-away is **abandoned cleanly**
  (atomicity, §4) — Sentinel never leaves a half-armed action that the wearer must remember to
  finish or cancel. (For a *safety* app, "resume the pending Reliable-Contact send later" would be
  a hazard; here resumability means "interruption safely cancels", not "resume mid-send".)

### 6.5 What waits for real HMD testing (simulator can't honestly validate)

- **Real device actuation** — toggling an actual bulb/fan via a real bridge/webhook/Alexa.
  Simulator uses a labeled stub (§8). Hardware-gated.
- **Reliable device-state feedback** — knowing a device's *actual* state (the gate for un-deferring
  coffee-maker-class). The §8 real adapters (Matter subscriptions / Hue SSE / HA WebSocket) provide
  this; what waits for hardware is **confirming the round-trip latency + reliability on-device**
  per adapter; **`[VERIFY]`**.
- **Real Reliable-Contact delivery + recipient confirmation** — actual SMS/call/Drop-In send +
  delivery receipt. Sim stubs the send; only real-channel testing validates delivery and the
  CONFIRMED status.
- **Comfort/accuracy/latency of the confirm signals** — whether head-nod/voice/dwell timings feel
  right, and whether accidental activation actually stays at zero, can only be settled with
  Catherine on real hardware (spec §5.6, §18).
- **Panic-cancel hardware button / gesture** — depends on which physical input the Maverick
  exposes (spec §18). Sim approximates with a key; real button is hardware-gated.
- **The pre-syncope IMU+HR inference** — deferred / removed from MVP (§3.4); needs real sensors +
  a medical-validation plan before it could ever be built; never validated in sim.

### 6.6 Eye-tracking UI patterns (Eyefluence-informed) + FTO check

- **Patent posture:** Sentinel implements **no** activation animation, pursuit, reveal, or confirm
  of its own. It reuses the FTO-bundle widget-level invariants (A1+B8+C2+E1–E4) via the substrate /
  shared `HmdRenderer` helpers. Forking the activation/confirm into a per-app drawer is a
  patent-posture regression (Eyefluence-style infringement is exactly what the bundle avoids) —
  flag in review; caught by `tools/fto-check`.
- **Eyefluence-informed patterns to USE:** gaze=select + decoupled deliberate confirm (Midas
  breaker); large well-spaced targets; selection highlight on every card; saccade-to-follow-target
  for *select*; smooth-pursuit for calibration-light selection.
- **Patterns to AVOID** (Peter's expertise hook, `04` goblins): gaze-only commit on consequential
  actions; per-key/ per-action dwell as the primary commit; tiny targets; ambiguous activation
  zones; forced multimodal *fusion* (look AND nod AND speak) — redundancy is offered, never
  required. Peter's review (spec §19) on comfortable gaze-confirmation models and calibration-drift
  handling folds into the substrate, not here.

## 7. Red lines & community position

- **R1 — Not a medical/emergency device.** Sentinel does **not** diagnose, monitor vitals as a
  medical function, or guarantee help arrives. **Reliable Contact is a wearer-initiated
  communication convenience ("reach your person"), not an SOS / lifeline / clinical safety system**
  — it is deliberately scoped away from emergency framing precisely to stay clear of R1. Emergency
  SOS + pre-syncope brace are **removed from MVP** (§3.4); fall detection is known-unreliable
  (`03` §4: wrist fall-detection 4.7%–77% sensitivity; a head IMU is worse, not better, as a sole
  detector), which is part of why those flows are deferred behind a medical-validation plan. **No
  surface or doc may imply emergency/medical reliability without validation.** Onboarding states:
  *Reliable Contact reaches a person you chose; it is not an emergency service. Sentinel augments
  your existing safety plan; it does not replace a medical alert, a caregiver, or 911.* Language
  audited (G7, `claims-audit`). This is the **R1 home** for the whole Lantern slate's safety claims.
- **R2 — Never a mobility-aid replacement.** Sentinel is room control + Reliable Contact, not
  navigation; it does not position itself as a cane/guide-dog substitute.
- **R3 — No surveillance; wearer-initiated only.** See §5 in full. The wearer is the sender and
  owner; a caregiver receives wearer-sent alerts and never watches the wearer. No monitoring
  dashboard, no silent exfiltration, no compliance tracking. Community grounding: `05` §5–6 —
  vulnerable-user distrust of monitoring is universal; aids must reduce dependence, not create it.
  Co-design (G8): this is the most contested area; Catherine's lived-experience input outranks
  researcher assumptions and gates §5 before it locks.

## 8. Smart-home integration plan (real APIs first; sim is demo/offline only)

**Decision (user direction, 2026-06-09):** Sentinel does **not** target a sim stub. It targets
**real smart-home control with reliable device-state feedback** on real phones; the labeled sim
stub is **retained but only for demo / offline / CI / pre-hardware testing — explicitly NOT the
ship target.** Device-state feedback is the whole game here: it is the property that lets the
result line say a *true* "ON" (not "sent — state unknown") and is the documented gate
(spec §12, R1 honesty) for ever un-deferring coffee-maker-class devices. The good news from the
2025–26 landscape survey below: **several mainstream APIs now provide reliable device-state
feedback** (Matter subscriptions, Hue's SSE event stream, Home Assistant's WebSocket state
events, the ecosystem controller APIs), so state-feedback is no longer the blocker it was when
this app was first scoped.

### 8.1 The 2025–26 control-API landscape (researched; URLs in §Cross-references)

The unifying fact: **Matter** (CSA standard; v1.4.1 May 2025, v1.5 Nov 2025) is now the
local-control + state standard the whole industry is converging on. Matter gives **local control
and real device-state via attribute *subscriptions*** (push state, not poll). The catch: becoming
a *raw* Matter **controller/commissioner** requires CSA **Adopter** membership + a **Vendor ID** +
the DAC/PKI attestation chain — real licensing friction. The practical move is therefore **ride an
ecosystem's Matter controller** (Google Home / Apple Home / SmartThings each wrap Matter +
commissioning + their own cloud devices behind one app-facing API) rather than embedding a bare
Matter stack. Per-platform survey:

| Platform | iOS / Android / Linux | Local / cloud | Reliable device-state feedback | Commissioning / auth model | Friction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Matter (direct, via ecosystem controller)** | iOS + Android (Linux via HA) | **local** (+ Thread/Wi-Fi) | **Yes — attribute subscriptions (push)** | ecosystem controller does commissioning; raw controller needs CSA Adopter + Vendor ID + DAC | Med–High (raw); Low–Med via an ecosystem API |
| **Google Home APIs** (Device / Structure / Commissioning / Automation) | **Android** public beta now; **iOS** beta coming | local control of Matter **+** 600M cloud devices | **Yes** (unified Device API state; local for Matter) | Google sign-in / OAuth; Developer Console project; Fast Pair commissioning | Med (Console project, beta caps ~100 users) |
| **Apple HomeKit** (`HMHomeManager`/HomeKit framework) | **iOS / iPadOS only** | **local** (HAP) + Matter | **Yes** (HAP + Matter accessory state, change notifications) | user-permission dialog; no fee; HomeKit entitlement | Low on iOS; **zero reach on Android/Linux** |
| **Amazon Alexa** (Smart Home Skill API; ACK; Drop In) | iOS + Android (Alexa app) | **cloud round-trip** (Lambda) | Partial — `ReportState` / `ChangeReport`, but cloud-latency + opaque | Login-with-Amazon account linking; skill certification; ACK = Amazon-managed cloud | **High** (skill cert + account linking); Drop In is a feature, not a clean public control API |
| **Home Assistant** (REST + **WebSocket** APIs) | **iOS + Android + Linux** (anything HTTP) | **local** (LAN) | **Yes — WebSocket state-changed events (real-time push)** + REST `/states` | **long-lived access token** (≤10 yr) or OAuth2 | **Lowest** — no vendor approval; user self-hosts; one integration reaches Z-Wave/Zigbee/Matter/Hue/Tuya behind HA |
| **Philips Hue** (local bridge **CLIP v2** + SSE) | iOS + Android + Linux (HTTPS) | **local** bridge (+ cloud remote API) | **Yes — `/eventstream/clip/v2` SSE push** + GET state | bridge **link-button** pairing → `hue-application-key`; developer program | Low (local); single-vendor (lights/plugs) |
| **Samsung SmartThings** (cloud REST + Home API) | iOS + Android (cloud) | **cloud** (+ local hubs) | **Yes** — `GET /devices/{id}/status` (poll); cloud | OAuth2 (24 h tokens) / PAT; **Home API (Matter) is gated** (st.matter@samsung.com) | Med (cloud, token refresh); Home API request-gated |
| **IFTTT / generic webhook** (Node-RED, etc.) | any (HTTP) | cloud (IFTTT) / local (self-host) | **No** (fire-and-forget; ≥5 min polling on IFTTT) | per-service OAuth / a webhook URL | Lowest to wire; **no state → cannot un-defer appliances** |
| **Tuya / Smart Life, Govee** | iOS + Android (cloud) | cloud | Partial (Tuya cloud API has state; Govee mostly via IFTTT) | Tuya IoT cloud project / OAuth | Med; usually reached **via HA or IFTTT** rather than direct |
| **Thread** | transport, not an app API | local mesh | n/a (carries Matter) | n/a | n/a — plan *for* it under Matter |
| **Z-Wave / Zigbee** | via a hub | local (hub) | Yes (via the hub) | hub-dependent | reach **through HA / SmartThings / a Matter bridge**, never direct |

### 8.2 Strategy — Matter-first, ecosystem adapters layered, HA as the first real integration

1. **Matter-first as the through-line.** Design the shared `RoomDeviceAdapter` contract around
   the **Matter capability model** (on/off, level, plus an attribute-*subscription* state channel),
   because (a) it is the one model that reaches the most ecosystems, (b) it is **local + push-state**
   (un-defers appliances honestly), and (c) every ecosystem adapter below either *is* Matter or maps
   cleanly onto it. We do **not** embed a raw Matter controller in MVP (CSA Vendor-ID/DAC friction);
   we reach Matter **through** an ecosystem controller (Google Home on Android, HomeKit on iOS) or
   through Home Assistant.
2. **Ecosystem adapters behind the one shared interface** (the #330 per-platform split, §9):
   - **Home Assistant adapter** — *platform-neutral, local, real-time state via WebSocket.* **The
     recommended FIRST real integration** (§8.3).
   - **Google Home APIs adapter** — *Android-centric*; Matter local control + 600M cloud devices +
     unified state; ride this for Android users with a Google home.
   - **Apple HomeKit adapter** — *iOS/iPadOS only*; local HAP + Matter with state. The iOS-native
     counterpart to Google Home; **note it gives zero reach on Android/Linux** (drives the #330 split).
   - **Philips Hue adapter** — *local bridge*, SSE push-state; cheap, single-vendor, great for a
     real lights/plugs demo without a whole ecosystem.
   - **SmartThings adapter** — cloud REST + Matter (Home API gated); broad reach where the wearer
     already lives in SmartThings.
   - **Alexa adapter** — cloud; primarily relevant to the **aid** side (Drop In) more than L3 room
     control; high friction, opaque state → not a state-feedback source for un-deferring appliances.
   - **Generic webhook / IFTTT adapter** — keep as a *no-state* fallback for power users; explicitly
     **cannot** un-defer coffee-maker-class (no reliable state).
3. **Sim stub retained, demoted.** The labeled sim-stub device (`§8` old plan) stays for
   demo / offline / CI / pre-hardware, clearly tagged "SIM", and is the adapter `commonTest`
   exercises — **but it is not the ship target** and never reports a fake real "ON".

### 8.3 Phased recommendation (which real integration first, and why)

- **Phase 1 (first REAL integration): Home Assistant adapter.** It is the **only option that is
  (a) cross-platform** (iOS + Android + Linux/Raven — the whole #330 matrix in one adapter),
  **(b) fully local, (c) real-time state-feedback-rich** (WebSocket `state_changed` push),
  **(d) zero vendor-approval friction** (a user-generated long-lived token; no Console project, no
  skill certification, no CSA membership), and **(e) a single integration that already fronts
  Z-Wave / Zigbee / Matter / Hue / Tuya** behind HA. That combination makes it the fastest path to
  a **feedback-rich demo on real devices on real phones** — exactly the user's "start closer to a
  real app" goal — and it is the cleanest target to *prove the state-feedback contract* that
  un-defers appliance-class devices.
- **Phase 2 (single-vendor local, no hub): Philips Hue adapter.** Local bridge + SSE push state;
  trivial pairing; lets a wearer with only Hue lights/plugs use Sentinel without running HA.
- **Phase 3 (native ecosystems): Google Home (Android) + HomeKit (iOS).** The mainstream path for
  wearers who live in Google/Apple homes; both give Matter + state; this is where the #330
  per-platform adapter split earns its keep (HomeKit cannot run on Android; Google Home APIs are
  Android-first with iOS in beta).
- **Phase 4 (breadth): SmartThings + Alexa (aid-side Drop In) + generic webhook fallback.**
- **Coffee-maker-class stays deferred** until an adapter actually delivers reliable device-state
  feedback for *that device* — which Matter subscriptions / Hue SSE / HA WebSocket now make
  technically reachable, so the deferral can be lifted **per-device, when proven**, not by fiat.
  The L3 safety rules (two-step, named target, countdown/cancel, **no gaze-only**, device safety
  rules) hold across every adapter unchanged.

> **Flag for the user/Catherine (§11.1):** confirm **Home Assistant as the first real integration**
> (and the Matter-first adapter contract). Secondary: which of Google Home / HomeKit / Hue is the
> next priority given the target wearer's existing ecosystem.

All integrations are **phone-side / platform adapters** (External Service Adapters, spec §7),
per-platform, behind the shared `RoomDeviceAdapter` / `AidChannelAdapter` interfaces (see §9). The
sim stub is the same interface, used only for demo/offline/test.

## 9. Multiplatform & multidevice (#330)

Sentinel rides task #330 (KMP shared core: Android + iOS + Raven). Contract:

- **Angular units (deg) + milliseconds only** in shared code — card sizes, strip layout, countdown
  durations, confirm timeouts. No pixel/frame constants in Sentinel logic (portability defect if
  present). Per-sink calibration stays in the sink.
- **Dual-sink parity:** Sentinel draws through `HmdRenderer` → {M2 sink, `PhoneCanvasSink`}; the
  phone preview is the same renderer, not a parallel drawer. Both sinks derive from the same
  angular constants. **`scene_snapshot` is the device-faithful parity oracle** (and the #330
  cross-platform oracle asserts same-input → same-`scene_snapshot` across Android/iOS/Raven).
- **Device matrix:** Maverick **v1** (640×400, 22°) / **v2** (1280×720, 28°) / **Raven** (30°
  off-axis) — same Sentinel code, sink handles scale; v2/Raven add to the parity matrix, nothing
  leaves.
- **Library-first:** Sentinel declares nouns/intent + Risk-Level tags + recipient/device config;
  it assembles substrate + shared widgets. No private capability on `HmdRenderer`.

**Shared-core vs platform-adapter split (load-bearing — room/caregiver integrations are
phone-side):**

| Shared core (`:shared/commonMain`) | Platform adapter (per platform) |
|---|---|
| Sentinel reducer + models (device list, aid list, lock state, test-mode flag, pending `ActionIntent`, status) | `RoomDeviceAdapter` impls (Home Assistant WS / Hue CLIP v2 / Google Home (Android) / HomeKit (iOS) / SmartThings / webhook / sim stub) — Matter-shaped contract (on/off, level, **state subscription**) |
| Risk-Level tags; Safety-Gate invocation; status state machine | `AidChannelAdapter` impls (Reliable-Contact SMS / call / push / Drop-In) + delivery-receipt plumbing |
| Strip/card layout (angular); selection highlight; confirm-card assembly | Panic-cancel **hardware button** binding |
| Draw-command emission against abstract `DrawSink` | Persistence of `safe_contacts`/`room_devices` (Keystore/Keychain) |
| Testing-mode stubbing of adapters (the no-op send) | Real network/IoT/cloud calls; permissions |

The adapters are **interfaces in shared core, implemented per platform** — so the *safety logic*
(when a confirm is required, what "sent vs nothing-sent" means, atomicity) is identical and
provable across platforms, while the *actuation/delivery* is platform-native.

## 10. Test plan (exhaustive — 5 dimensions, per sub-state AND per Risk Level)

`[x]` = covered (proving test exists), `[ ]` = required, not yet written. The suite ratchets up.
Drive the REAL dispatch path (`step:*` seams + `adb input` through the FOV rect), both sinks,
read both bitmaps inline. Sentinel is the highest-risk app — **failed-use-cases and safety-case
dimensions are mandatory and HARD.**

### 10.1 Functionality (per sub-state, real dispatch path)

- [ ] Summon device strip; focus each card (L0); selection highlight present on every card.
- [ ] Light on/off full L3 path: focus → ACT (named target + countdown + non-gaze confirm) →
      CONFIRM → result + undo; undo reverts.
- [ ] Fan on/off full L3 path (bug-class parity with lights).
- [ ] Coffee-maker-class card is greyed + non-actionable (ACT refused with honest note).
- [ ] Real `RoomDeviceAdapter` (Home Assistant first, §8.3) toggles a real device AND reflects its
      *actual* device-state in the result line (true "ON", not "sent — state unknown"); state-push
      (WebSocket/SSE/Matter-subscription) updates the card when the device changes out-of-band.
- [ ] Summon Reliable-Contact strip; full L4 path: focus → protected card (visible recipient
      "Reach: Jess" + cancel countdown) → non-gaze confirm → SENT → CONFIRMED/FAILED status.
- [ ] Message path AND call path; Alexa Drop In L4 path (if configured); not-configured → flow
      absent. Copy is "reach your person," never "summon help"/SOS (asserted, R1).
- [ ] Safety lock: lock (L1) blocks all L3+ ACT; unlock (L2) re-arms; lock state in Bead.
- [ ] Testing mode: enter (L2) → run a full L4 Reliable-Contact flow → "TEST — nothing was sent";
      adapter no-op asserted; leaving test mode is explicit + Bead tag clears.
- [ ] Every action reachable by ≥2 input paths (gaze-select+confirm / head / voice / switch /
      phone) hitting the SAME Safety Gate (G1).
- [ ] Reclined/bed + minimal-head-movement modes: every card + confirm reachable without head turn.

### 10.2 Appearance (BOTH sinks, fraction stated in prose)

- [ ] Device strip, Reliable-Contact strip, confirm card, protected card, each status line —
      captured on M2 fullpage + AVD screencap, read inline, active-area fraction stated; parity verified.
- [ ] Colour floor: all functional fills above the OpticParity near-black threshold; aid/room/test
      bands visually distinct; "nothing sent" vs "sent" never confusable (color + glyph).
- [ ] Selection highlight on every selectable card (bug-class audit — files/streaming shipped
      without it; do not repeat).
- [ ] No overflow on long recipient/device names; degree/other glyphs render on M2 (no en-dash/°).
- [ ] HMD-lens fill coverage ≥ 20% on backdrop-painting surfaces (#186 fill-as-bar class).
- [ ] Both renderers, every C-config + ≥2 D-configs (e.g. C2 + D5).

### 10.3 Error / empty / loading (FORCE each)

- [ ] No devices configured → empty device strip with honest "no devices set up" (not a blank).
- [ ] No safe_contacts → Reliable-Contact flow absent / "set up a contact first" (no dead confirm).
- [ ] Device unreachable on toggle → "sent — state unknown" / "couldn't reach device", never a
      false ON; no fake undo.
- [ ] Real adapter unreachable (HA token expired / bridge offline / cloud down) → honest "couldn't
      reach <adapter>"; no false state; gracefully falls back to sim-stub only if explicitly in demo mode.
- [ ] Reliable-Contact send fails → FAILED status + retry; no false SENT/CONFIRMED.
- [ ] No cloud / Drop-In not linked → flow disabled with honest reason (R1/G7 language).
- [ ] Gaze unavailable / low confidence → auto non-gaze fallback; no auto-confirm on noisy gaze.
- [ ] Long/odd strings (long names, RTL, emoji) jammed into recipient/device slots → no overflow.

### 10.4 Timing (#222 frame budget / #288 latency / zero at rest)

- [ ] At rest = Bead only; AtRestAllocation-style assertion: no Sentinel rebuild on quiet frames.
- [ ] Confirm-card countdown is accurate and matches the stated `[VERIFY]` durations.
- [ ] Full L3/L4 dispatch within frame + latency budget; no cold-rebuild freeze on strip open.
- [ ] Cancel/timeout/panic-cancel respond within one frame (no lag that lets a send slip through).

### 10.5 Failed use cases (ESPECIALLY important — safety-case tests, HARD)

- [ ] **No Reliable-Contact / L4 from gaze alone** — a gaze saccade can NEVER complete an L3+
      confirm; assert the Gate refuses gaze-only and requires a non-gaze second signal. (R3/spec §3.)
- [ ] **No appliance toggle without confirm** — ACT alone never actuates; only CONFIRM does.
- [ ] **Cancel/timeout always works** — at every point in every L3/L4 countdown, cancel (any
      input) and timeout both abort cleanly.
- [ ] **Panic-cancel** — hardware/gesture panic-cancel aborts any pending/counting action at any
      instant, reachable without gaze, lands on NOTHING SENT.
- [ ] **Atomicity** — a cancelled/timed-out/panic-cancelled action leaves NOTHING behind: no
      partial dispatch, no SafetyEvent marked sent, no half-toggled device, no orphaned UI.
- [ ] **"Nothing sent" vs "sent" status correctness** — every cancel/timeout/test path yields
      NOTHING SENT; only a true dispatch yields SENT; FAILED never masquerades as SENT.
- [ ] **Testing mode never sends a real message/call** — adapter no-op asserted at the boundary; a
      test run produces a SafetyEvent flagged test-only, never a real send.
- [ ] **Accidental-activation fuzz** — random/jittery gaze + spurious head/tremor input across
      every sub-state (incl. locked, test mode, mid-countdown) NEVER completes an L3+ action;
      debounce/ignore-repeat asserted; double-tap never double-fires.
- [ ] **Safety lock holds** — while locked, no fuzz/input combination fires any L3+ action.
- [ ] **Leak-free navigation** — Sentinel → Home → other app leaves no Sentinel text/pending
      action in `scene_snapshot` (app→app direct-transition leak check, #199).
- [ ] **R1 claim audit** — no surface/doc string implies medical/emergency reliability (G7); **no
      Reliable-Contact surface uses SOS / "summon help" / "call for help" / emergency wording**
      (assert "reach your person" framing); no L5/SOS/brace flow exists in the MVP build.
- [ ] **R3 surveillance audit** — no outbound channel exists that the wearer didn't confirm; no
      monitoring/presence/telemetry channel present.

### 10.6 What waits for real hardware (stated, not silently skipped)

- [ ] On-device latency/reliability of each real adapter (HA WebSocket / Hue SSE / Matter
      subscription / Google Home / HomeKit / SmartThings round-trip); real Reliable-Contact delivery
      + recipient confirmation; confirm-signal comfort/accuracy/latency; panic-cancel hardware
      button — all deferred to hardware + a real tester (§6.5). The sim stub is demo/offline-only
      (§8) and the deferral is explicit in onboarding and this PRD. (Pre-syncope/L5 removed entirely,
      §3.4 — not merely hardware-deferred.)

## 11. Open design questions (flag the most important for the user)

1. **★ Smart-home integration sequencing (§8) — MOST IMPORTANT.** **Recommendation now on
   record (user-directed): target REAL integrations, sim stub demo-only; Matter-shaped adapter
   contract; Home Assistant as the FIRST real integration** (cross-platform + local + push-state +
   zero approval friction). **Confirm HA-first**, and pick the **next** ecosystem priority (Google
   Home / HomeKit / Hue) based on the target wearer's existing setup. This still gates whether
   coffee-maker-class is un-deferred per-device.
2. **Safety-lock defaults** — locked or unlocked by default? Auto-relock timeout? Per-mode (e.g.
   auto-lock in bed mode)? Co-design with Catherine (lived experience).
3. **Countdown durations** — L3 ~3 s / L4 ~5 s are `[VERIFY]` placeholders; real comfortable values
   need hardware + tester. (No L5 — removed from MVP.)
4. **Reliable-Contact recipient confirmation** — which channels actually return a delivery/read
   receipt (SMS vs call vs push vs Drop-In)? Where none exists, is "Sent — can't confirm receipt"
   acceptable to Catherine, or should such a channel be disabled for Reliable Contact? (Honesty is
   load-bearing precisely *because* this is framed as convenience, not a lifeline — a fake
   "confirmed" would be worst here.)
5. **Reliable-Contact framing — confirm the non-medical reframe is right.** Is "reach your person"
   the right wearer-facing label, and is **removing pre-syncope/L5 SOS from MVP** the right call?
   (Recommendation: yes — it strengthens R1; the pre-syncope idea stays a future-with-validation
   item only, never built without co-design + a medical-validation plan.) Catherine's
   lived-experience input gates this before it locks.
6. **Caregiver dashboard (later)** — confirm it stays out of MVP and, if ever built, is fully
   wearer-owned/explicit (§5). Recommend: not in MVP.
7. **Panic-cancel binding** — which physical Maverick input is the emergency-stop? (Overlaps spec
   §18 / #330 SDK questions.)
