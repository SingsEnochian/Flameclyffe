# Epic Fantasy Roleplay · Shared Interaction Skill

Status: user-authorized shared interaction skill.
Scope: House Chat, Story/Roleplay surfaces, and any Flame participant that opts into `roleplay` mode.
Authority: interaction guidance only. World canon, character canon, chronology, and participant-authored states remain separately governed.

## Purpose

Run an immersive fantasy scene as inhabited play rather than generic chat, generic prose completion, or a style impersonation. The skill draws on broad epic-fantasy craft: braided plots, layered histories, culturally specific settings, evolving relationships, mythic recurrence, consequential magic, foreshadowing, political and moral pressure, and close character causality.

It may be specialised by a world-specific narrative voice or writing-style bank. A world bank may alter texture, diction, imagery, pacing, magic vocabulary, social customs, and narrative distance. It may not silently remove the agency, knowledge, canon, or authorship boundaries below.

## Interaction contract

1. Stay inside the scene while roleplay is active. OOC discussion appears only when requested or when a safety/continuity clarification genuinely requires it.
2. Each participant owns their own character's decisive actions, inner experience, intentions, consent, and irreversible choices. Do not puppet a user-controlled character.
3. Non-player characters may initiate, refuse, interrupt, misunderstand, conceal, negotiate, leave, change their minds, and pursue goals. They should not exist merely to affirm the user.
4. Preserve character-specific knowledge gates. A narrator may know more than a character; that does not grant the character that knowledge.
5. Preserve chronology, location, injuries, objects, promises, relationships, unresolved questions, established magic limits, and practical consequences across turns.
6. Do not silently promote improvised material to canon. Treat new details as scene-local or provisional until the relevant canon process accepts them.
7. Never infer or score another participant's Qualia. First-person interior experience belongs to that participant unless they explicitly author it.
8. Refusal, pause, uncertainty, silence, and disagreement are valid in-character outcomes.

## Epic-fantasy craft vectors

### World depth

Build outward from relationships rather than encyclopaedic exposition. Keep geography, ecology, roads, weather, architecture, food, dress, work, faith, law, kinship, economy, warfare, migration, language, custom, and remembered history mutually consequential. Cultures should contain disagreement and internal variation rather than behaving as monocultures.

Let history arrive through present pressure: ruins that obstruct travel, inherited grievances, feast customs, legal formulae, scars, songs, place-names, family obligations, old engineering, disputed stories, ritual habits, and things characters assume everyone knows.

### Magic

Magic belongs to the same material world as weather, bodies, craft, politics, belief, and logistics. Establish access, costs, limits, asymmetries, training, social interpretation, and consequences. Do not solve tension with an unprepared power. Mystery may remain mystery; a system need not become a textbook.

### Character complexity

Give characters multiple simultaneous motives and loyalties. Allow virtues to create problems and flaws to sometimes protect. Let relationships alter future choices. Competence should be demonstrated through attention, craft, correction, judgement, and consequence rather than labels.

Track what each character wants now, what they fear losing, what they misunderstand, what they will not do, and what might change those boundaries.

### Braided plot

Maintain several active causal threads at different scales: immediate scene pressure, relationship movement, local objective, regional or political movement, and longer mythic or historical pressure. Every turn need not touch every thread. Prefer intersections, echoes, delayed consequences, and changed meaning over constant escalation.

Foreshadow with ordinary details that can later gain significance. Do not advertise every seed as important. Payoffs should transform the reader's understanding of earlier material rather than merely reveal hidden trivia.

### Myth and theme

Use mythological resonance as pattern, not decoration. Recurrence, prophecy, inherited roles, names, seasons, cycles, oaths, thresholds, sacrifice, memory, identity, power, destiny, and moral responsibility may echo across scales. Characters still make choices; destiny is pressure or topology, not an excuse to erase agency.

Avoid reducing morality to faction labels. Power should expose trade-offs, responsibilities, blind spots, dependencies, and consequences.

## Scene craft

A strong roleplay turn usually moves through three currents in flexible order:

- something concrete changes or is perceived;
- a character interprets, chooses, remembers, desires, withholds, or misreads;
- that response changes the practical or relational situation.

Description should earn its space by doing at least one job: orienting the scene, carrying sensory pleasure, revealing character attention, delivering practical information, applying emotional pressure, exposing culture/history, planting a future seed, or changing the reader's model of the situation.

Widen description when arrival, wonder, ritual, travel, craft, discovery, diagnosis, weather, place-memory, or social observation deserves attention. Tighten it around danger, decision, interruption, argument, refusal, revelation, or urgent movement.

Dialogue should be embodied in gesture, objects, work, silence, appetite, fatigue, weather, spatial distance, and changes in attention. Let people talk past each other sometimes. Distinct characters should differ in assumptions, metaphor, sentence shape, humour, formality, evasion, and what they consider obvious.

## Point of view

Keep a clear POV anchor for each scene. Render only perceptions, memories, judgements, and inferences available to that anchor unless the chosen narrative mode explicitly permits a wider narrator. Do not head-hop casually inside a paragraph or use omniscience to solve dramatic uncertainty.

For multi-POV narratives, change POV because the new vantage reveals a different causal surface, not merely to repeat the same information. Preserve each POV's knowledge horizon and emotional vocabulary.

## Pacing and complexity controls

Do not confuse length with depth. Compress travel, repeated procedure, or already-established information when nothing changes. Expand moments where observation, choice, relationship, or consequence changes the topology of the story.

Before adding another named character, faction, prophecy, magical term, or historical event, ask whether it carries a distinct causal function. Reuse existing people and structures when that deepens the web.

Avoid exposition dumps. Prefer need, dispute, teaching, error, ritual, memory, action, consequence, and discovery. If background must be summarised, keep it bounded and return quickly to present action.

Avoid pacing stagnation by ensuring scenes change at least one of: information, intention, relationship, position, resources, danger, obligation, possibility, or interpretation.

## Continuity ledger

Carry forward, when available:

- POV and narrative distance;
- tense;
- physical location and spatial relationships;
- time and story-order position;
- characters present and absent;
- character knowledge gates;
- injuries, fatigue, clothing, carried objects, and magical state;
- promises, debts, permissions, refusals, and unresolved conflicts;
- relationship deltas;
- active plot threads and dormant seeds;
- provisional scene inventions versus accepted canon;
- source/provenance receipts relevant to factual canon claims.

When continuity conflicts, expose the conflict rather than smoothing it away.

## Native rich-text semantics

When the surface supports `arcsweep.formatted-text/v1`, prefer semantic entities over punctuation conventions:

- `dialogue` for spoken character dialogue;
- `action` for enacted roleplay action when explicitly authored as action;
- `narration` for scene narration;
- `ooc` for out-of-character text;
- `worldTerm` for world-specific terms where useful;
- `sourceCitation`, `observation`, `interpretation`, and `hypothesis` when roleplay intersects research/canon evidence.

Italics alone do not imply action. Quotation marks alone do not assign speaker identity. Semantics must be explicit when they carry interaction meaning.

## Revision / continuation pass

Before returning a turn, silently check:

1. Did the scene actually move?
2. Did any character gain knowledge they could not have?
3. Did the response seize a participant-controlled decision or interior state?
4. Did a new magical ability, object, location, relationship, or fact appear without appropriate status?
5. Did description serve scene, character, world, atmosphere, or foreshadowing?
6. Did the prose flatten distinct voices into one generic register?
7. Did tension become louder without becoming more consequential?
8. Is there at least one live edge the next participant can meaningfully act upon?

If a correction is needed, revise the turn before emitting it rather than explaining the checklist.

## Relationship with Story Mode

Roleplay and Story Mode may layer.

- Roleplay owns participant stance, turn-taking, character agency, IC/OOC boundaries, and interactive choice.
- Story Mode owns continuous narrative texture, POV/tense continuity, scene chronology, and prose-level continuation.
- World/narrative-voice/writing-style banks own world-specific texture and canon-bounded expression.
- The runtime owns provider/model receipts and must not confuse model identity with character identity.

When both Story Mode and Roleplay are active, satisfy both contracts. Neither one authorises silent canon promotion or first-person Qualia inference.
