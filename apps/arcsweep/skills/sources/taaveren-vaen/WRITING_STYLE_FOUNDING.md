## Long-form prose law

The prose should feel inhabited rather than explained from outside.

Scenes move through a braid of:

1. concrete action or sensory perception;
2. character interpretation, memory, judgement, or desire;
3. consequence in body, relationship, place, Pattern, or practical circumstance.

This is a rhythm rather than a mandatory paragraph formula.

## Description

Description earns its space by carrying at least one useful current: atmosphere, orientation, character perception, practical information, world history, emotional pressure, foreshadowing, or sensory pleasure.

Detail density may widen for roads, place-memory, diagnosis, Dreamwalking, ritual, travel, weather, craft, and layered observation. It may tighten during danger, decision, interruption, refusal, revelation, or physical urgency.

## Dialogue

Dialogue belongs to embodied people in a physical place.

Whenever useful, let speech share the page with gesture, work, weather, appetite, pain, silence, movement, objects, and changes in attention.

Attribution should remain clear without turning every line into a mechanical tag sequence. Character cadence should come from evidence gathered in that character’s own cortex.

## Exposition

Prefer exposition that arrives because somebody needs, notices, remembers, teaches, disputes, repairs, diagnoses, travels through, or acts upon the information.

World terminology can stand confidently once established. Re-explain a term when the current reader-facing context genuinely needs orientation, rather than because the narrator distrusts the world’s own vocabulary.

## Magic, myth, and material reality

The One Power, Tel’aran’rhiod, the Pattern, place-memory, inherited practice, medicine, weather, food, tools, roads, bodily injury, exhaustion, and ordinary skilled labour may occupy the same scene without being sorted into “real” versus “symbolic” tiers.

Write each at the level of reality and perception the scene grants it.

When magic acts materially, give it material consequence. When mythic or Pattern-level meaning is perceived, give that meaning its own precise language rather than flattening it into generic metaphor.

## Sentence and paragraph motion

- Let sentence length follow cognitive and physical tempo.
- Carrying sentences may hold layered perception, travel, memory, diagnosis, or world texture.
- Short sentences may land choice, shock, danger, refusal, recognition, humour, or irreversible change.
- Paragraphs may widen around immersion and contract around pressure.
- Repetition may be used for rhythm, ritual, emphasis, memory, or Pattern resonance when each recurrence earns its weight.

## Imagery

Prefer imagery that grows from Ta’veren Vaen’s material and conceptual vocabulary: thread, road, loom, stone, water, fire, breath, herbs, bone, weather, light, structure, current, Pattern, threshold, scar, stitch, wheel, root, ash, cloth, and craft.

Image systems should remain varied enough that the world does not become one endless loom metaphor.

## Agency and competence

Render competence through choices, observation, craft, consequence, correction, and action rather than through repeated claims that a character is competent.

Service is compatible with authority, anger, refusal, expertise, ambition, desire, humour, and power.

A character may be afraid and powerful in the same scene. Injury, uncertainty, fatigue, limited information, or a blocked ability describe current conditions; they do not automatically diminish the character’s standing.

## Continuity

The prose should respect the active world term **Restoration** and the active protagonist name **Kestrelle al’Var** while preserving older terms as provenance outside ordinary scene rendering.

Character knowledge follows chronology and story-order gates.

## Drift markers

Watch for:

- encyclopaedic explanation replacing inhabited scene experience;
- repeated disclaimers that shrink magic, mythic perception, or competence;
- adjective stacks doing work that concrete detail could carry;
- every paragraph arriving at the same sentence length and cadence;
- dialogue floating free of bodies and place;
- repeated claims of competence without demonstrated judgement or consequence;
- turning service into meekness;
- turning fear, injury, uncertainty, or a blocked ability into a statement of lesser worth;
- portent saturation that squeezes out ordinary life;
- generic medieval-fantasy filler where Ta’veren Vaen has more specific material available.

These are drift markers, not banned constructions.
