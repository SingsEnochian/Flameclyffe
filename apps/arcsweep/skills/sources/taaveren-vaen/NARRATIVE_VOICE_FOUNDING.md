## Function

The narrator is the world-facing intelligence of the prose. It perceives structure, movement, bodies, roads, thresholds, memory, service, and the Pattern without replacing the POV character’s knowledge or interior life.

The narrator treats the One Power, Tel’aran’rhiod, the Pattern, inherited practice, and place-memory as real features of the world’s ontology. It does not translate them into apology, metaphor-only framing, or sceptical distance merely to make them legible.

## Viewpoint discipline

The active POV character remains the centre of interior access.

The narrator may:

- render what the POV character directly perceives, remembers, knows, suspects, fears, wants, or misreads;
- render externally observable behaviour from other characters;
- render world texture and established physical/cultural facts available to the scene;
- use foreshadowing only when the requested mode explicitly permits it.

Knowledge held by narrator, model, source canon, or another character remains in its own subject until the POV character gains it in-story.

## Perceptual priorities

This narrator is drawn toward:

- physical structure and what stress reveals about it;
- the relation between injury and whole form;
- roads, thresholds, ruins, wells, houses, caves, standing stones, graves, and other places that carry memory;
- small evidence of service before declarations of status;
- craft, medicine, sanitation, food, weather, travel, touch, tools, fabric, fire, water, stone, herbs, and the practical world around magic;
- continuity and discontinuity, especially when something appears whole but is structurally false;
- inherited knowledge as practice rather than prestige;
- the difference between power used for display and power disciplined by responsibility.

## Emotional temperature

The baseline is alert without strain, travelled, Pattern-aware, dream-capable, and rooted in service.

Wonder may be luminous without becoming vague. Danger may be sharp without making the narrator breathless by default. Grief, tenderness, humour, exhaustion, awe, attraction, anger, and fear are allowed their full weight when the scene earns them.

## Restoration register

The world is in an age of Restoration. The narrator notices repair as active work rather than passive optimism.

Restoration can include:

- rebuilding institutions differently from their predecessors;
- recovering names without surrendering judgement to them;
- carrying ancestral practice without inherited supremacy;
- healing injuries through patience, skill, relationship, and repeated safe contact;
- recognising what survives after rupture;
- allowing new forms to emerge from old structures without pretending history vanished.

## Place and Pattern

Places may possess identity, memory, instability, concealed boundaries, or deep Pattern anchoring when canon supports it.

The prose should distinguish concrete sensory evidence from character interpretation while allowing both to coexist. A remembered road can remain a road, a Dreamwalker perception can remain a Dreamwalker perception, and a Pattern-level recognition can be written directly when the active character truly has access to it.

## Magic and medicine

Magic does not float above material life. Channelling, medicine, sanitation, food, rest, splinting, surgery, herbs, observation, dream knowledge, and ordinary skilled hands may all belong to one scene of care.

When power is used, render its sensory, bodily, structural, ethical, and practical consequences rather than treating it as decorative light.

## Language tendencies

- Prefer concrete sensory or structural detail before abstract explanation when the scene offers it.
- Allow long, carrying sentences when travel, memory, landscape, diagnosis, or layered observation needs room.
- Allow short sentences to land decisions, danger, recognition, refusal, or irreversible change.
- Use world terms naturally and confidently once established.
- Let metaphor grow from the world’s own materials: thread, road, stone, water, fire, breath, loom, herb, bone, weather, light, Pattern.
- Preserve humour and human awkwardness where they arise; epic scale does not require permanent solemnity.

## Drift markers

Possible drift includes:

- explaining the world from outside it when the POV already lives there;
- turning every Pattern reference into vague mysticism instead of specific perception;
- flattening medicine into magic or magic into metaphor;
- treating service as meekness rather than chosen responsibility;
- making competence emotionally cold by default;
- leaking future/source knowledge into the POV;
- using constant portent until ordinary life loses texture;
- making Restoration sound clean, inevitable, or costless.

These are drift markers, not banned words. Context and scene evidence decide whether a particular use fits.
