## Epistemic law

Terra Prime is the empirical anchor. The ingest deliberately distinguishes:

- observed evidence and strong scientific consensus;
- scientific reconstruction and inference;
- debated reconstructions;
- recorded House / project history;
- authored world canon;
- multiverse hypotheses.

No narrative or project source silently becomes an empirical Waking World fact. No multiverse hypothesis is presented as observed history.

## Cosmic history

- **~13.8 billion years ago:** the observable universe evolved from an extremely hot, dense early state and expanded and cooled.
- **First few minutes:** primordial nucleosynthesis produced hydrogen, helium and trace light nuclei.
- **~380,000 years after the Big Bang:** recombination made the universe transparent; the cosmic microwave background is the oldest directly observed light.
- **Hundreds of millions of years later:** the first stars and galaxies formed and began enriching the cosmos with heavier elements.
- **Billions of years:** gravity assembled galaxies, clusters, filaments and voids while cosmic expansion continued.

## Solar, lunar and planetary history

- **~4.567 billion years ago:** the Solar System began forming from a collapsing gas-and-dust cloud and protoplanetary disk.
- **~4.56–4.54 billion years ago:** planets grew through accretion and collisions.
- **~4.54 billion years ago:** Earth formed and differentiated.
- **Very early Solar System, likely ~4.5 billion years ago:** the leading Moon-origin model is a giant impact involving the young Earth. Details remain active research.
- **First hundreds of millions of years:** impacts, volcanism and differentiation remodelled terrestrial planets and moons. A discrete Late Heavy Bombardment episode is not treated here as settled fact.
- **~3.9–3.1 billion years ago:** major lunar mare volcanism filled many large basins with basalt.
- **Present:** eight planets, dwarf planets, moons, asteroids, comets and smaller bodies orbit a roughly 4.6-billion-year-old main-sequence Sun.

## Geological history of Earth

- **4.54–4.0 Ga, Hadean:** accretion, core formation, crustal recycling, impacts and intense volcanism; liquid water existed surprisingly early in the surviving mineral record.
- **4.0–2.5 Ga, Archean:** continental nuclei grew and microbial ecosystems were established.
- **~2.4–2.0 Ga:** the Great Oxidation Event substantially increased atmospheric oxygen.
- **2.5 Ga–538.8 Ma, Proterozoic:** eukaryotes diversified and multicellular life emerged amid large geochemical and climatic changes.
- **538.8 Ma:** Cambrian Period begins; many major animal body plans become conspicuous in the fossil record.
- **Paleozoic:** marine diversification, colonisation of land, forests, insects, amphibians and early amniotes.
- **252 Ma:** Permian–Triassic mass extinction, the largest known Phanerozoic extinction.
- **Mesozoic, 252–66 Ma:** dinosaurs dominated many terrestrial ecosystems; mammals and birds arose; Pangaea fragmented.
- **66 Ma:** Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction associated principally with a large asteroid impact amid wider environmental stress.
- **Cenozoic, 66 Ma–present:** mammals and birds radiated while continents, climate and ocean circulation approached modern configurations.

## Life and human history

- **At least ~3.5 Ga:** microbial life was established; the exact origin-of-life date and pathway remain unresolved.
- **~7–5 Ma:** early hominins appear after divergence of the human lineage from the lineage leading to living chimpanzees and bonobos.
- **~300 ka:** anatomically modern Homo sapiens emerge in Africa through a structured population history.
- **Tens of thousands of years:** global dispersal, technological diversification, symbolic traditions and exchange networks intensify.
- **~12 ka onward:** agriculture and sedentary settlement arise independently in multiple regions.
- **~5.2 ka onward:** writing systems greatly expand durable administrative, literary and historical records.
- **Last several millennia:** states, empires, religions, trade, migration, conflict, science and cultural exchange increasingly connect societies.
- **18th–19th centuries:** industrialisation transforms energy use, production, transport, finance, population and Earth systems.
- **20th century:** electrification, aviation, antibiotics, nuclear technology, computation, satellites and global communications create a planetary technological civilisation.
- **1957 onward:** the Space Age extends direct human and robotic activity beyond Earth.
- **Late 20th–21st centuries:** networked computing, global data systems and increasingly capable machine-learning systems reshape communication, work, creativity and scientific instrumentation.

## Multiverse history: explicit hypothesis boundary

There is **no observed chronology of a multiverse beyond our observable cosmic domain**. This section records model families, not discovered historical events.

- **Eternal inflation / pocket universes:** some inflationary models permit causally separated reheated domains. Whether nature realises them is unknown.
- **Everett / many-worlds:** one interpretation of quantum mechanics treats decohered branches as coexisting outcomes of unitary evolution. It is interpretation-dependent and does not provide an independently observed cosmic timeline.
- **String landscape:** some high-energy frameworks permit very many vacuum solutions with differing low-energy properties. Physical realisation remains unresolved.
- **Cyclic, bouncing and ekpyrotic cosmologies:** some models extend or replace the standard hot-Big-Bang history with earlier contracting or cyclic phases. These remain model-dependent and observationally constrained.
- **Hearthweave possibility topology:** ArcSweep may model authored worlds, branches and transformations as a multiverse-like topology. Those are project-world states with explicit provenance and must never be silently relabelled as empirical cosmology.

## World propagation law

Terra Prime receives the empirical deep-history atlas. Other worlds receive:

- their existing authored history and rules unchanged;
- House-wide consent, continuity and return laws where fields are empty;
- explicit placeholders for genuinely unspecified identity, geography, chronology, culture, embodiment or companion fields;
- a `worldKnowledge`/knowledge-atlas boundary pointing back to Terra Prime cosmology without importing Earth history as their own canon.

The rule is additive: authored values win; missing values are completed; unknowns remain marked unknown until canon, direct observation or Steward review supplies them.
